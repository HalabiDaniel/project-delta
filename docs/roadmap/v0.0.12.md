## Overview

v0.0.10 gave every player a stable, secure identity and put PostgreSQL in place with migrations,
the full schema and an idempotent transaction ledger (phases 1 to 3, see
[PERSISTENCE.md](../PERSISTENCE.md)). The game world is still kept in memory: when the server stops
or a player leaves, their base, paper, planes and progress are gone.

This version makes everything a player builds **theirs, for good**. The central decision (the
developer's, 2026-11-02) is that **a base belongs to the player, not to a desk**:

- A player builds their base over time: buildings, levels, damage, the hangar and its planes, the
  paper store and its paper.
- When they join a session they are given a random free desk, and their whole base is placed on
  it exactly as they left it.
- When they leave, the base is packed up and kept. The next session places it on another random
  desk, so a player sometimes plays from a quiet corner and sometimes from the busy middle.

v0.0.12 builds the persistent, portable base and everything that travels with it. v0.0.13 builds
the sessions it is placed into (time limits, several sessions at once, the territory lifecycle and
the AI filling empty desks). v0.0.14 puts the whole system online.

The release has six central objectives:

1. A portable base owned by the player, saved in PostgreSQL and placed on any desk.
2. Every paper change through the idempotent ledger, so no reward can be paid twice.
3. Production only while the player is online in a session, and a slower economy overall
   (dearer planes, slower paper).
4. Planes, the hangar, missions, statistics and tutorial progress saved with the player.
5. Fairness timers (protections, shields, cooldowns, kill windows) that follow the player and
   survive restarts.
6. Usernames, chosen once by each player, used everywhere a name is shown ("Daniel's desk").

Preserve the PlayCanvas client, the shared TypeScript rule classes, the authoritative Node and
Colyseus server and the v0.0.10 identity and store. Single-player keeps its local save on Home Desk.

## 1. The Portable Base

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** One base per player, saved, and rebuildable on any of the 16 desks.

### A. Model

- A base is owned by the player (their UUID), per game mode (multiplayer only; single-player keeps
  its local save).
- It holds: the buildings in each slot (kind, level, health, per-building data), the paper store's
  health, base statistics, the hangar level and its planes, and the paper balance with its
  capacity and protected reserve.
- Slots, runway and installation positions are already laid out relative to the desk the base sits
  on (`homeDefenses` and `homeHeading` in `src/defenses/Layout.ts`), and `Base` already saves and
  loads itself (`BaseSave`, `Base.load`). A base is placed by building `Base` on the assigned
  desk's `Territory` and loading the saved state into it.

### B. Database

- A new migration re-keys the base tables to the player: `bases` (one row per player) and
  `base_buildings` (player, slot), replacing the territory-keyed `buildings` table from 0001
  (unused so far, so nothing is lost).
- `territories` stays, but from v0.0.13 it records **occupancy during a session** (which desk a
  player's base is on right now), not ownership.
- Damage is saved and stays until repaired, wherever the base goes.

### C. Placing and packing

- **Placing:** when a session gives the player a desk (v0.0.13), the AI's installations on that
  desk are removed, the base is built from the save, and the player is told where they are
  ("This session: Daniel's desk, back left corner").
- **Packing:** when the player leaves for good (after the reconnect grace and after any raid on
  them has settled, see v0.0.13), the base's current state is saved and its installations are
  removed from the world.
- A base is never in two places: the server refuses to place a base that is already placed.
- A brand-new player's first base is the starting base (`Base.startFresh`), saved at once.

### D. Missions after a move

- The mission board is checked against the new location when the base is placed
  (`MissionDirector.validate`): cards that no longer make sense (a target now next door, a
  territory that is now the player's own) are replaced, not paid.

## 2. Paper and the Ledger

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Every change to a player's paper is a row in the transaction log, applied at most once.

- Every paper change (mission reward, bounty, building, upgrade, repair, refund, folding and
  refitting a plane, raid reward and loss, production) goes through `Store.applyTransaction` with
  an idempotency key built from its cause: `mission:<id>`, `raid:<id>:reward`,
  `raid:<id>:loss`, `build:<request id>`, `prod:<session>:<minute>` and so on.
- The rule classes stay as they are: `ResourceManager` keeps deciding what a change is; the server
  writes the change to the ledger and applies the confirmed balance.
- The same key twice (a repeated message, a browser refresh, a reconnect, a retry after a restart)
  changes nothing.
- A spend that the database refuses (it would go below zero) is refused in the game too.
- Capacity and the protected reserve are saved with the balance.
- The balance can always be explained from the log (tested).

---

## 3. Production and the Economy Retune

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Paper is earned by playing, and planes are a real decision again.

### A. Production only while playing

- Workshops produce **only while the player is online in a session**. There is no offline
  production and nothing to collect on return.
- Production stops the moment the player's connection drops and resumes when they are back in a
  session.
- Production is credited in small steps (for example once a minute) through the ledger, capped by
  storage as today (`HomeIncome`).

### B. Retune (provisional values, all in `src/config.ts`)

| What | Change |
|---|---|
| Folding a plane (every aircraft) | Dearer: about 1.5 to 2 times today's cost, scaled by class (fighters less, bombers and special planes more) |
| Refits and rebuilds | Scaled with the folding cost |
| Base production per minute | Slower: about two thirds of today's rate |
| Workshop bonus | Slower in proportion |
| Emergency Swift | Stays free and is never stored as an asset |

- The aim: a lost plane is felt, and a player can't keep folding new planes without flying missions.
- The developer adjusts the aircraft stats themselves later; this version changes prices and
  production only.
- The balance tests (`npm test`) and the v0.0.6 simulation harness (`src/director/Simulation.ts`)
  are updated so a typical session's income still allows building and replacing planes at a
  steady, slower pace.

## 4. Planes and the Hangar

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** The planes a player owns are saved with their base.

- Saved per plane: type, health and condition (ready, recovered, damaged, in repair), the saved
  loadout and the quick-rebuild selection, in the `aircraft` table.
- A destroyed plane stays destroyed (a database trigger already refuses to bring one back).
- The free emergency Swift is never stored.
- The selected aircraft and the hangar level are saved.
- A plane in the air when the player leaves follows the flight rules in v0.0.13 section 5.
- Customisation fields stay empty placeholders for v0.0.21.

---

## 5. Missions, Statistics and Tutorial Progress

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** A returning player finds their board, history and progress as they left them.

- The mission board, swaps left, history and the director's adaptive state
  (`MissionDirector.DirectorState`) are saved, so a refresh or a new session never rerolls the
  board.
- Mission outcomes are written once, with the mission id as the idempotency key: a mission never
  completes twice.
- Statistics (sorties, kills, deaths, assists, raids, defenses, missions, score) are saved per
  player, lifetime. Per-session scores belong to v0.0.13.
- Tutorial steps and first-time tips are saved on the server for multiplayer players.

---

## 6. Fairness Timers

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** No restart, reconnect or session change resets a protection or opens a farming window.

- New-player protection, the raid shield, pair cooldowns (who raided whom), the repeat-raid factor
  and the kill-farming window are stored as **real clock times on the player** (and raid and kill
  records in `pvp_records`), not as session times.
- They follow the player from session to session and through restarts.
- A server restart never shortens or lengthens them.

---

## 7. Usernames

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Players choose a name once, and it is shown everywhere.

- On their first join a player chooses a **username**: 3 to 16 letters, digits or `_`, unique
  across the game, checked against a block list.
- It replaces the free display name: scoreboard, name tags, kill feed and desk labels.
- Desks are named after whoever is on them: **"{username}'s desk"** for a player. AI-held desks get
  random goofy names ("Billy's desk", "Tina's desk", "Big Greg's desk"), picked per session from a
  list in `src/config.ts`.
- A username can be changed with a cooldown (provisional: once a week); ownership never depends on it.
- Players are still guests (device token, invite or transfer code). Accounts with sign-in arrive in
  v0.0.22; guests and signed-in players play together, and a guest keeps their username and base
  when they upgrade.

## 8. Testing and Verification

**Priority:** Critical

- All existing tests pass.
- New tests, against both stores (memory and PostgreSQL):
  - a base saved, packed, and placed on every one of the 16 desks with identical buildings, levels
    and damage;
  - a base can't be placed twice;
  - every paper change in a session is in the ledger and the balance equals the log;
  - the same reward, refund and loss keys applied twice pay once, including after a restart;
  - production runs only while connected, stops on a drop and never credits offline time;
  - the new prices and production in the balance tests and the simulation;
  - planes, hangar, board, history, statistics and tutorial flags survive a restart;
  - fairness timers survive a restart and a session change;
  - usernames: uniqueness, the block list, the cooldown, desk labels.
- Bots (`server/bots/`) leave and rejoin, and get their base back.

## Final Design Principle

A base is the player's own long-term project. Wherever a session puts it, it arrives exactly as
they left it: every turret, every scorch mark, every plane in the hangar and every sheet of paper.
