# paperwars.io: Version 0.0.22 Development Plan

**Development stage:** Closed alpha (preparing for public access)
**Release type:** Infrastructure, operations and compliance milestone
**Release theme:** Accounts, Matchmaking, Moderation and a Production Foundation
**Builds on:** [v0.0.21](v0.0.21.md) (Customization and social)
**Followed by:** [v0.0.23](v0.0.23.md) (Admin dashboard)

Part of the [roadmap to v0.1.0](README.md).

---

## Overview

Up to version 0.0.21 paperwars.io has been played by the developer, an invited friend and a small invited group. They are known, trusted and few. The private identity and invite system from v0.0.10, usernames (v0.0.12), sessions on one server (v0.0.13) and the Railway setup from v0.0.14 are enough for them.

Version 0.1.0 opens the game to strangers. Strangers need real accounts, a way to find a good session wherever they are, protection from abuse, a clear legal basis for handling their data, and a server setup that keeps working when one machine fails.

Version 0.0.22 builds all of that. It is mostly invisible to players when it works, which is the point.

The release has eight central objectives:

1. Add public accounts alongside guests: email or OAuth sign-in, recovery, guest upgrade and account deletion. Guests and signed-in players play together.
2. Add matchmaking that places players into suitable sessions across servers and regions, and opens new sessions automatically.
3. Run sessions at scale: many sessions on several servers, draining servers cleanly for deploys.
4. Set data-retention rules for inactive players (their bases are never in a session while they are away, v0.0.12).
5. Provide moderation: a name filter, a report queue, moderation actions and an audit log, as server APIs (the screens are the admin dashboard, v0.0.23).
6. Detect and limit abuse: multiple accounts, reward farming and cheating clients.
7. Add privacy-light analytics and crash reporting, and the legal basis for them (privacy policy, terms of service, GDPR compliance).
8. Move to production infrastructure: several game servers, staging, automated deploys, backups with tested restores, monitoring, and load tests.

The release must preserve the PlayCanvas client, the TypeScript game rules shared between client and server, the Node and Colyseus server, and PostgreSQL. The server stays authoritative for everything that matters (GDD §16.2).

Do not add new gameplay systems in this version. Balance changes are allowed only where matchmaking requires them.

## 1. Public Accounts

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Let anyone create an account, keep it secure, recover it, and delete it.

### A. Sign-in methods

| Method | Notes |
|---|---|
| Guest | Immediate play, no details required; progress tied to a device token |
| Email | Passwordless magic link or email plus password with secure hashing (Argon2id or bcrypt) |
| OAuth | Google and Discord to start; others later |

- Guests must be able to play within about a minute (GDD §18, criterion 1). Guests and signed-in players share the same sessions; nothing in a session depends on which one a player is.
- A guest can upgrade to an email or OAuth account at any time and keeps their username, base, planes, paper, progress and inventory.
- One account may link several sign-in methods.

### B. Sessions and security

- Short-lived access tokens and rotating refresh tokens in secure, HTTP-only cookies.
- The Colyseus join flow verifies the token server-side; the client never chooses its own player ID.
- Rate limits on sign-in, sign-up, magic-link and recovery endpoints.
- Email verification before an account can send friend requests or reports.
- Secrets in environment variables or a secret manager, never in the repository.

### C. Recovery

- Email-based recovery for email accounts.
- OAuth accounts recover through their provider.
- Guest accounts are warned that clearing browser data loses progress, and prompted to upgrade after their first meaningful progress.

### D. Display names

- Usernames (v0.0.12) stay unique across the game, filtered (section 5) and changeable with a cooldown.
- Ownership and identity always use the account ID, never the display name.

### E. Account deletion and data export

- Players can delete their account from settings. Deletion removes personal data and their base, and anonymises historic records needed for other players' battle reports.
- Players can export their personal data (GDPR right of access).
- Deletion and export requests are logged and completed within the legally required time.

### F. Migration from the private alpha

- Private alpha players (v0.0.10 device tokens, v0.0.12 usernames and bases) keep everything by linking an email or OAuth sign-in; until they do, they carry on as guests.
- Migration is tested on a copy of the real alpha database before it runs.

## 2. Matchmaking

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Put every player into a session where they can have a good time, without waiting.

### A. Session directory

- The servers keep a shared registry of open sessions: region, layout and environment (v0.0.20),
  players and capacity, time left, and how welcoming it is to new players.
- The lobby from v0.0.17 recommends sessions first (Quick join), with an option to browse.

### B. Placement rules

Matchmaking scores open sessions by:

- Free desks and time left (joining closes near the end, v0.0.13).
- Region and measured latency.
- Strength balance (avoid placing a new player into a session full of veterans).
- Friends, session allies (v0.0.19) and squad members (v0.0.21) already in the session, within
  the alliance and squad limits.

Inside the session, placement stays random (v0.0.13): matchmaking chooses the session, never the desk.

### C. Opening and closing sessions

- When recommended sessions fill up, a new session opens on a server with spare capacity, in the
  player's region.
- Sessions with too few players are kept lively by AI desks and AI aircraft (GDD §18, criterion 7:
  engaging activities at low population); a session is never merged mid-way, players simply move to
  the next session when it ends.

### D. Rejoining

- A player inside the reconnect grace always rejoins their session and desk first (v0.0.13).
- A player is in at most one session at a time.

## 3. Sessions at Scale

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Many sessions on several servers, run without the developer's attention.

- Several game servers per region, each hosting several sessions; the registry (section 2A) is
  shared through the database or a small shared cache.
- A server can be **drained** for a deploy: it opens no new sessions, lets running sessions reach
  their end (or ends them with notice after a maximum wait), then restarts.
- A base is placed in at most one session across all servers (the database refuses a second
  placement, v0.0.12).
- Session length, capacity and the lifecycle timers (v0.0.13) stay configuration and are tuned in
  the v0.1.0 test rounds.
- Session results (v0.0.13) are kept for statistics and support; cosmetic end-of-session awards
  (top defender, top raider, best newcomer) are granted idempotently by the server.

The seven-day campaigns of GDD §3.3 are replaced by these timed sessions and portable bases
(decided 2026-11-02): nothing resets except what lives only in a session.

---

## 4. Inactive Players

**Priority:** Medium
**Goal:** Clear rules for players who stop playing.

- An absent player holds nothing in any session: their base is packed when they leave (v0.0.12),
  so the GDD §3.4 territory-release rules are no longer needed.
- Guests inactive for a long time (provisional: 12 months) have their data removed after a notice
  in the game; signed-in accounts are kept until deleted, as stated in the privacy policy
  (section 8).

## 5. Moderation

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Give the developer the tools to keep the game friendly once strangers arrive (GDD §12.4).

### A. Name and banner filter

- Display names, squad names and banner text are checked against a block list and pattern rules (including common letter substitutions) when set.
- Flagged names are rejected with a clear reason, or sent for review when the case is unclear.

### B. Reports

- Players can report another player from the scoreboard, kill feed, recent players or friends list.
- Report reasons: offensive name, cheating, harassment or targeting, exploit abuse, other.
- Reports include automatic context (session, time, recent encounters between the two players).
- Duplicate reports are grouped. Reporters are rate-limited.

### C. Moderation actions (server side)

The actions below are built as server APIs here; their screens are the admin dashboard in
[v0.0.23](v0.0.23.md):

- Report queue with filtering and status (open, actioned, dismissed).
- Player lookup: accounts, sign-in methods, sessions, recent activity, flags.
- Actions: warn, rename, mute emotes, kick, temporary ban, permanent ban, revoke items, restore items.
- Session tools: inspect a session, close it to new players, end it early with notice, remove a player from it.

### D. Audit log

- Every admin action is recorded with the admin, time, target, reason and before and after state.
- The audit log is append-only and kept separately from game data.

### E. Player protection

- Mute and block (v0.0.19 and v0.0.21) are always available and work immediately.
- The mission director never offers missions that repeatedly target the same player against their will; cooldowns from v0.0.9 still apply.

## 6. Abuse Prevention

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Make cheating, farming and multiple accounts unprofitable and detectable (GDD §13.6).

### A. Multiple accounts

- Signals: device tokens, IP address ranges, timing patterns and repeated interactions.
- Accounts suspected of being linked cannot earn rewards from each other and cannot be in the same session at once.
- Signals are for detection and review, not automatic permanent bans.

### B. Reward farming

- Existing rules (decaying raid rewards, assist and participation thresholds, bounty rules from v0.0.9 and v0.0.19, squad rules from v0.0.21) are reviewed together.
- New server checks flag accounts whose reward rate is far above normal, or who repeatedly lose to the same opponent.

### C. Cheating clients

- The server already decides positions, hits and rewards. Add validation for input frames that imply impossible turn rates, speeds or fire rates.
- Repeated violations are logged, flagged and, past a threshold, disconnect the player.
- Never trust the client for damage, rewards, ownership or mission success.

### D. Network abuse

- Rate limits per connection for messages and commands.
- Message size limits and schema validation for every message in `src/net/protocol.ts`.
- Limits on concurrent connections per account and per IP.

## 7. Analytics and Crash Reporting

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Learn whether the game works for players, while collecting as little data as possible.

### A. Principles

- Collect only what answers a specific question.
- No third-party advertising trackers.
- Event data uses the account ID or a pseudonymous ID, never names or emails.
- Respect consent where required; analytics that need consent are off until given.

### B. Events

| Area | Examples |
|---|---|
| Funnel | Page loaded, first flight, first mission complete, first return visit |
| Retention | Day 1, day 7 and day 30 returns, session length |
| Gameplay | Mission offers and choices, results, aircraft picked, causes of loss |
| Economy | Resource sources and sinks per session |
| Technical | Load time, FPS buckets, disconnects, errors |

### C. Crash and error reporting

- Client errors and unhandled promise rejections are captured with the build version and browser.
- Server errors are captured with session and room context.
- Personal data is scrubbed before sending.

### D. Dashboards

- A small dashboard covering the success criteria in GDD §18 that can be measured.
- Used in v0.1.0's test rounds.

## 8. Legal and Compliance

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Have a clear legal basis for running a public online game.

### A. Documents

- Privacy policy: what is collected, why, how long it is kept, who processes it, how to delete or export data.
- Terms of service: acceptable use, account termination, virtual items having no cash value, liability.
- Cookie and storage notice where required.
- Licence attributions (already in the credits from v0.0.17).

### B. GDPR

- Lawful basis for each type of data.
- A record of processors (hosting, email, analytics, crash reporting, OAuth providers).
- Data retention periods enforced by scheduled jobs.
- Right of access, rectification, erasure and portability (section 1.E).

### C. Age

- Decide the minimum age and how it is communicated (the developer decides with legal advice).

### D. Aircraft names and likenesses

The 13 aircraft are folded-paper interpretations of real aircraft, and several names echo real
aircraft nicknames (for example Raptor, Lightning and Lancer). Before strangers play:

- Review every aircraft name and silhouette for trademark and likeness risk, with legal advice
  where needed. Manufacturers and governments have protected aircraft names and designs before.
- Keep every model a stylised folded-paper interpretation, not a replica: no manufacturer names,
  logos, insignia, serial numbers or official markings on models, cosmetics, store pages or
  marketing.
- Do not use the real designations (F-22, B-2 and so on) in game text, marketing or metadata; the
  inspirations stay in internal design documents only.
- Keep a list of fallback names for any aircraft whose name is judged risky, and rename before
  v0.1.0 if needed. Renaming touches only display strings; aircraft ids in `src/config.ts` and
  persisted records stay stable.

### E. Review

- The developer approves all legal texts and the aircraft name review. The agent drafts structure and technical facts, not legal advice.

## 9. Production Infrastructure

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Run the game reliably for many players across several machines.

### A. Environments

| Environment | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Local | Development (`npm run dev`, `npm run server`) |
| Staging | Same setup as production, used for every release before it goes live |
| Production | Public sessions |

### B. Game servers

- Several Colyseus server processes, each hosting several sessions.
- A matchmaker or presence service (Colyseus presence with Redis, or equivalent) that knows which process hosts which session.
- Worlds can be drained from a server for maintenance without ending them abruptly.
- Regional deployments where v0.0.20's region support requires them.

### C. Deploys

- Automated build, test and deploy pipeline on every merge to the release branch.
- Database migrations run automatically and are reversible or have a tested rollback plan.
- Client and server versions are checked at connect; mismatches show the v0.0.17 version-mismatch screen.

### D. Backups and restore drills

- Automated daily full backups and continuous write-ahead log archiving for PostgreSQL.
- Backups stored separately from the primary database.
- A restore drill is performed and documented before release: restore to staging, verify a session and accounts.

### E. Monitoring and alerts

- Health checks for game servers, the database and the matchmaker.
- Metrics: players online, sessions, tick time, message rate, memory, CPU, database latency, error rate.
- Alerts to the developer for outages, error spikes and backups failing.

### F. Operations documentation

- Runbooks for: deploy, rollback, restore from backup, drain a server, end a session early, rotate secrets, respond to an outage.

## 10. Load Testing

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Know how many players and sessions the system can handle before strangers arrive.

### A. Tooling

- Extend `server/loadtest.ts` and the bots in `server/bots/` to run many sessions and many bots at once against staging.
- Include sign-in, matchmaking, joining, flying, combat, missions, raids and disconnects.

### B. Targets (to confirm with the developer)

- A defined number of concurrent sessions per server process at full population.
- Server tick time stays within budget at that load.
- Matchmaking places a player in under a few seconds.
- No data loss under a simulated server crash.

### C. Results

- Results are published in the README's measurements section, including hardware and settings.

## 11. Testing and Verification

- Automated tests for account flows, token checks, guest upgrade, deletion and export.
- Automated tests for matchmaking scoring, session creation and the squad limit.
- Many short sessions run end to end on several servers, including a server drained for a deploy mid-session.
- Inactive-guest data removal tested with simulated clocks.
- Moderation and admin actions tested, including the audit log.
- Abuse checks tested with bots that cheat on purpose (impossible turns, fire rate, farming patterns).
- Restore drill completed on staging.
- Load tests completed and recorded.
- Invited group playtest of the full public flow: sign up, matchmaking, play, session end, next session.

## 13. Developer Responsibilities

- Choose hosting, database, email and OAuth providers, and create accounts for them.
- Provide or approve the privacy policy, terms of service and minimum age, with legal advice where needed.
- Decide on the aircraft name and likeness review, and approve any renames.
- Approve the session tuning for public play, the inactive-guest rule and the moderation policy.
- Act as the first moderator and test the moderation actions (from the command line until the v0.0.23 dashboard).
- Approve load-test targets.

## Final design principle

A stranger should be able to arrive, play, and come back next week to find their progress safe and the game fair. If a server fails, nobody should lose anything that matters. If someone behaves badly, the developer should be able to see it and act on it.
