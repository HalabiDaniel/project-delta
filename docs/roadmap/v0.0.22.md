# paperwars.io: Version 0.0.22 Development Plan

**Development stage:** Private alpha (expanding invited group)
**Release type:** Content milestone
**Release theme:** Maps: nine places to fight in, indoors and outdoors, each one room or area, with furniture to fly through
**Builds on:** [v0.0.21](v0.0.21.md) (advanced multiplayer) and [v0.0.11](v0.0.11.md) (art integration and the classroom)
**Followed by:** [v0.0.23](v0.0.23.md) (world conditions)

Part of the [roadmap to v0.1.0](README.md). Added on 2026-11-06 (see
[the decisions](README.md#decisions-of-2026-11-06-world)).

---

## Overview

Every session so far takes place in one classroom: 16 desks in a 4 × 4 grid (v0.0.11). The
classroom works, but after a few sessions players know every corner of it.

Version 0.0.22 turns the classroom into **one map of nine**. Each map is **one room or area**
(not several connected rooms), at the same toy scale: the players' planes are paper, and the
furniture is the landscape. Maps are either **indoor** or **outdoor**, and that difference matters:
indoor maps have a ceiling, machines and room lighting; outdoor maps have an open sky, and in
v0.0.23 they get weather and a day and night cycle.

| | Indoor | Outdoor |
|---|---|---|
| Maps | Classroom, Science Lab, Library, Cafeteria, Gym, Bedroom | Playground, Backyard, Rooftop |
| Top of the map | A ceiling | An altitude limit under an open sky |
| Light | Room lights (and, in v0.0.23, lights out) | The sun (and, in v0.0.23, day and night) |
| Moving air (v0.0.23) | Fans, vents, radiators, open windows | Wind, gusts and thermals everywhere |
| Water (v0.0.23) | Rare indoor sprinklers | Rain showers, garden sprinklers |

This version builds the maps themselves: their layout, territories, landmarks, obstacles and the
gaps planes can fly through. Everything that **moves or changes** in the world (fans that destroy
planes, wind, rain, sprinklers, day and night, lights out, bursting beakers) is
[v0.0.23](v0.0.23.md). The maps leave the places for it ready.

The release has six central objectives:

1. A **map framework**: a map is data (layout, territories, colliders, gaps, lighting, indoor or
   outdoor), and the classroom becomes the first map built on it, unchanged for players.
2. **Eight new maps**, five indoor and three outdoor, each with 16 territories for sessions of 16.
3. **Flying through furniture**: under desks and tables, between chair legs, through shelf gaps,
   tunnels and climbing frames, as fast but risky shortcuts and as cover from defenses.
4. **Sessions on any map**: a session opens on one map, and portable bases (v0.0.12) are placed
   onto that map's territories exactly as they were left.
5. Missions, the director, AI flight and defenses that work on every map.
6. The same visual quality and performance as the finished classroom, on every map.

Preserve:

- The portable base (v0.0.12): a base is the same on every map. Only the platform under it and the
  dressing around it change.
- Sessions, the territory lifecycle and the server's authority (v0.0.13).
- The art direction (`docs/ART_DIRECTION.md`), the asset pipeline (v0.0.11) and the v0.0.15 look.
- Every existing feature and its tests.

**Moved here:** the three new environments planned for World scale (bedroom, library, art room)
are part of this version's roster instead. The art room is not on the developer's list
(2026-11-06) and is dropped. The 32-player layouts stay in [v0.0.24](v0.0.24.md), which now enlarges each of these
maps.

## 1. The Map Framework

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Adding a map is adding data and models, not changing game code.

### A. What a map is

A map definition (`src/world/maps/`, one file per map) holds:

| Part | Contents |
|---|---|
| Identity | Id, name, short description, icon, indoor or outdoor |
| Space | The playable bounds, the floor, and the ceiling (indoor) or altitude limit (outdoor) |
| Territories | 16 positions with platform shape, height and the furniture they sit on |
| Landmarks and props | Per territory and for the room, with their colliders |
| Gaps | Tagged fly-through openings with their size (section 3) |
| Condition anchors | Where v0.0.23 puts fans, vents, windows, sprinklers, beakers and the like; inert here |
| Lighting | An indoor or outdoor lighting profile in the shared lighting model |
| Mini-map | The map's top-down art and outlines |
| AI | Navigation hints and flight plans for the map |
| Naming | The territory noun ("desk", "bench", "table"...) and the AI desks' goofy names |

### B. One set of colliders

Client, prediction and server read the same collider data from the map definition, as the
classroom's desks, landmarks and props already do (v0.0.11). Tests check that client and server
agree on every map.

### C. The classroom as map one

The classroom is rebuilt as the first map definition. Nothing changes for players: the same desks,
themes, landmarks and names. Every existing test that uses the classroom runs against the new
definition, and a screenshot comparison proves it looks the same.

### D. Territories and bases

- Every map has **16 territories**, one per player, like the classroom. v0.0.24 adds layouts for
  32.
- A territory is a **platform on a piece of furniture** (a lab bench, a cafeteria table, a slide's
  platform, an AC unit). The base's slots, runway and launch pad sit on the platform the same way on
  every map, so a portable base fits anywhere.
- **Labels:** "{username}'s desk" becomes "{username}'s {noun}", with the noun from the map
  ("{username}'s bench" in the lab). The developer decides whether to keep "desk" everywhere
  instead.
- Every territory gets a themed look per map (as the classroom's desks have since v0.0.11) and a
  readable ownership colour and symbol.

**Goal:** Nine places that each play differently, not nine skins of the classroom.

Names, territories and landmarks are provisional, for the developer to approve. "Hooks" are what
v0.0.23 brings to life on that map.

|---|---|---|---|---|

### C. Every map must

- Play differently: open (Gym, Rooftop) or cluttered (Lab, Bedroom), high or low, many gaps or few.
- Be readable from the air: territories, ownership colours and the home runway are clear at a
  glance, and the map's landmarks help players find their way.
- Keep runways, take-off paths, construction slots and stores clear (the classroom's placement
  tests, extended to every map), including clear space for the v0.0.23 hazards. **No hazard
  anchor may be placed near a runway, a launch pad or a base slot.**
- Give every territory a fair share: no desk where the nearest neighbours are much closer or much
  further than average, or where turrets can't see the approaches. A layout check measures it.

## 3. Flying Through Things

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Shortcuts and cover that reward skill and punish carelessness.

- **Gaps** are tagged in the map data with their opening size. Hitting the edge of a gap is a
  normal collision (damage by closing speed).
- **Size matters:** a plane fits through a gap if its collision sphere does. Small planes (Swift,
  Dart, Specter) fit through almost everything, big ones (Widewing, Ghost, Lancer) only through the
  large openings. That gives small planes a real niche on cluttered maps. The garage card shows
  how many of a map's gaps a plane fits through.
- **Cover:** furniture blocks defenses' line of sight. A raider who comes in low, under the
  tables, gets closer before the turrets react. Defenses already check line of sight against
  colliders; this version makes sure every map's furniture is in that check.
- **Maneuvers** (v0.0.16) check their path against the same colliders, so a Fold Back under a
  table is refused if there is no room ("No room").
- **AI** uses the gaps its plane fits through when its flight plan crosses them, and AI aces take
  low routes on raids.

## 4. Sessions and Maps

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** A session takes place on one map, and players know which one before they join.

- A session opens on a map. Which maps are in rotation is set in the config for now (the developer
  picks), and v0.0.26's matchmaking can choose between them.
- The session lobby (v0.0.19) shows each session's map with its name, picture and indoor or
  outdoor icon.
- A player's portable base is placed on a free territory of that map, exactly as they left it
  (v0.0.13's placement, on the new territory list).
- The session's map is saved with the session and survives a restart and crash recovery.
- The tutorial stays in the classroom.

---

## 5. Missions, AI and Defenses on Every Map

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Every system that knows about the classroom knows about every map.

- **Missions and the director:** mission targets, rings, recon points and delivery spots are
  placed per map (in the map data or generated from it), and every mission type works on every
  map. Missions that use gaps ("fly under the bleachers") appear where the map has them.
- **World events** (v0.0.20) find their routes on every map.
- **AI flight plans and navigation** (`src/ai/`) work from each map's navigation hints, and an AI
  never gets stuck flying into furniture. A test flies AI planes across every map.
- **Defenses** see and shoot with each map's line-of-sight colliders.
- **The mini-map** draws each map's outline and furniture.

## 6. Look and Performance

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Each map looks finished and runs like the classroom.

### A. Look

- Each map gets its own backdrop, lighting profile and palette within the art direction. Outdoor
  maps get a sky and a sun direction ready for v0.0.23's day and night.
- Landmarks and mission targets are custom models; furniture and scenery can use the developer's
  own models or a few owned assets, through the v0.0.11 import pipeline.

### B. Building order

1. **Greybox:** every map in simple shapes first, so its layout can be played and tuned before any
   art is made.
2. **Art pass:** models, materials and lighting, in batches, with screenshots at each checkpoint.
3. A map is not complete with placeholders. If a model is missing, the gap is documented and the
   map stays out of rotation.

### C. Performance

- Only the session's map is loaded, behind the loading screen (v0.0.19).
- Each map stays within the per-preset budgets (`?perf`, v0.0.11): draw calls, triangles and frame
  time measured on both test computers.
- Instancing and batching for repeated furniture (chairs, books, benches).

## 7. Testing and Verification

### A. Automated

- The classroom map definition gives the same desks, colliders and names as before, and every
  earlier test passes on it.
- For every map: client and server colliders agree; nothing blocks runways, take-off paths, slots,
  stores or hazard clearances; the layout fairness check passes; every gap is reachable and its
  size is right.
- Placement of portable bases on every map, including a full session and restart recovery.
- Every mission type can be generated and completed on every map.
- AI flight across every map without getting stuck; AI uses only gaps it fits through.
- Maneuver path checks against each map's furniture.

### B. Browser

- Screenshots of every map at the desk, in flight and on the mini-map, on High and Low.
- Bots playing a session on every map in headless Chromium.
- Frame time and draw calls per map on the presets.

### C. Playtests

- The developer, the friend and the invited group play every map in the greybox stage and again
  after the art pass.
- Questions: Does each map feel different? Is any territory unfair? Are the gaps fun or annoying?
  Can you find your way?

## 9. Developer Responsibilities

- Approve the map list, layouts, territory placements, names and landmarks (greybox checkpoint).
- Supply or approve the models for each map, or decide which owned assets to use.
- Decide the territory labels ("{username}'s bench" per map, or "desk" everywhere).
- Decide which maps are in rotation for the playtests.
- Play every map and report what feels unfair, confusing or dull.

## 10. AI Agent Implementation Strategy

| Phase | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Audit the classroom's world code, colliders and placement tests; the map definition format; the classroom rebuilt as map one with a screenshot comparison; the regression baseline |
| 2 | Sessions on a map: selection, saving, base placement, the lobby's map display |
| 3 | Greybox of the eight new maps with territories, colliders, gaps and condition anchors; layout and placement checks. **Checkpoint:** the developer flies every greybox |
| 4 | Missions, director, world events, AI navigation, defenses and the mini-map on every map |
| 5 | Art pass, first batch: Science Lab, Library, Cafeteria |
| 6 | Art pass, second batch: Gym, Bedroom, Playground |
| 7 | Art pass, third batch: Backyard, Rooftop; performance per map. **Checkpoint:** the playtest on every map |

- Work in reviewable increments, each with screenshots at a developer checkpoint.
- Do not mark a phase complete because the code compiles; verify it with tests, browser runs and
  play.
- Preserve every working feature from earlier versions.
- Record decisions and open questions in the README.

## 11. Definition of Done

- [ ] The classroom runs on the map framework with no visible change.
- [ ] The eight new maps are playable with 16 territories each, finished art and no placeholders.
- [ ] Every map is clearly indoor or outdoor, with its ceiling or altitude limit and lighting.
- [ ] Gaps work with plane sizes; furniture gives cover from defenses.
- [ ] A session opens on any map; bases are placed and saved correctly; the lobby shows the map.
- [ ] Missions, world events, AI and defenses work on every map.
- [ ] No map blocks runways, slots or hazard clearances, and every map passes the fairness check.
- [ ] Every map stays within the per-preset performance budgets.
- [ ] All earlier automated tests pass.
- [ ] The developer and the invited group have played every map.

---

## Final Design Principle

A player should know which map they are on from a single glance at the screen, and play it
differently from the last one. The furniture is the terrain: it should invite risky flying, not
just stand in the way.
