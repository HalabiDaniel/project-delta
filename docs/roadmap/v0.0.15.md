# paperwars.io: Version 0.0.15 Development Plan

**Development stage:** Private Playable Alpha (pre-release)
**Release type:** Gameplay depth milestone
**Release theme:** Three-Resource Economy, Anti-Snowball Balance, Mission Variety and Server-Side Progression
**Builds on:** [v0.0.14](v0.0.14.md) (menus, settings and branding)
**Followed by:** [v0.0.16](v0.0.16.md) (advanced multiplayer systems)

Part of the [roadmap to v0.1.0](README.md).

---

## Overview

By v0.0.14 paperwars.io is a persistent, deployed, two-player multiplayer game with its final art,
controller support, a rebuilt HUD, a new soundscape and finished menus. Its economy, however, is
still the single-resource economy designed for the v0.0.1 to v0.0.7 single-player prototype:
paper buys everything, ink and bombs are simply what a plane carries, and medals, records,
achievements and mastery live in the browser's local save.

The GDD (§4.2, §13) describes a richer economy with three resources, and the advanced multiplayer
systems in v0.0.16 (escorts, bounties, deeper raids) need that economy to be in place first:
escorts and bounties have to cost and pay something meaningful, and raid losses have to touch more
than one stock. Rebalancing an economy after the public release would be far more disruptive than
doing it now, while only invited testers are affected.

Version 0.0.15 has seven central objectives:

1. Turn **ink** and **rubber balls** into real territory resources alongside paper, with
   production, storage, costs, raid losses and protected reserves.
2. Add **upkeep and anti-snowball controls** so wealthy players gain options, not a runaway lead.
3. Broaden the **mission content**: stunts and exploration, timed challenges, more varied
   deliveries and multi-sortie operations that work in multiplayer.
4. Bring **world events, medals, records, achievements and aircraft mastery** into the shared,
   persistent multiplayer world, decided by the server.
5. Build **economy simulation tooling** that runs whole campaigns, so balance is measured rather
   than guessed.
6. Introduce the **radar signature and stealth system**: stealthier aircraft are detected later,
   never made invisible. The Falcon's dormant stealth trait from v0.0.11 activates.
7. Add two aircraft: the **Lightning** (F-35, stealth multirole) and the **Thug** (A-10, ground
   attack), bringing the roster to eight.

The release must preserve:

- The PlayCanvas, TypeScript and browser-based client. Do not migrate engines.
- The authoritative Node.js and Colyseus server introduced in v0.0.9.
- The PostgreSQL persistence, identity and recovery introduced in v0.0.10. Every persisted world
  and player from earlier versions must survive the upgrade.
- The art, controller, HUD, audio and menu work of v0.0.11 to v0.0.14. New panels use the v0.0.12
  design system; new sounds slot into the v0.0.13 audio system.

Do not expand scope into stealth bombers or drones (v0.0.16), escorts, bounties, rivalries or social features (v0.0.16), new
environments or larger worlds (v0.0.17), or cosmetics (v0.0.18).

## 1. Ink and Rubber Balls as Territory Resources

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Give every territory three resources with distinct roles, so that what a player builds,
flies and risks becomes a real decision rather than a single paper total.

### A. Resource roles

| Resource | Main uses | Produced by | Main losses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper | Aircraft construction, buildings, repairs | Base production, paper workshops, missions | Construction, repairs, raids, failed missions |
| Ink | Gun ammunition, rocket and turret ammunition, special weapons, markings | Ink structures (v0.0.11 model), missions, events | Firing, turret and launcher reloads, raids |
| Rubber balls | Bombs and heavy payloads, heavy anti-air rounds | Rubber stores or scavenging, missions, events | Bomb drops, heavy defense reloads, raids |

- Paper stays the dominant resource: the basic aircraft and the basic defenses must remain
  reachable with paper alone (GDD §4.5, §13.2).
- Ink and rubber balls must create choices (fly a bomber or a fighter, rearm turrets or build a
  plane), not bookkeeping. If playtesting shows a resource adds friction without decisions,
  simplify it rather than ship it.
- Do not add a fourth resource in this version (GDD §4.2: only if it improves decisions).

### B. Production and storage

- Generalize the paper-only income in `src/systems/HomeIncome.ts` and the balance in
  `src/systems/ResourceManager.ts` into a per-resource model: rate, capacity, protected reserve and
  boosted recovery below the reserve, for each resource.
- Production is computed by the server from elapsed time and capped by storage, using the
  validated offline-production rules from v0.0.10. No client ever reports its own income.
- Storage buildings (from v0.0.7 and the v0.0.11 models) gain per-resource capacity; the ink
  structure becomes a real building in the base slot system (`src/base/Base.ts`).
- Building levels, health and damage affect each resource's production and capacity the same way
  they affect paper today.
- Show all three resources with the icon-and-number style of the v0.0.12 HUD (production,
  capacity, protected amount) in the economy strip and base management.

### C. Costs and consumption

- Aircraft construction costs (`AIRCRAFT` in `src/config.ts`) gain ink and rubber components where
  the role justifies it: the Widewing costs rubber for its payload, fighters cost ink for their
  guns. The Swift (the starter) and the free emergency Swift remain obtainable without ink or rubber.
- Rearming becomes a real cost: refilling a plane's ink and bombs at home draws on the territory's
  stocks. A plane can always launch with a basic minimum load so nobody is grounded by an empty
  ink store.
- Defensive installations consume ink (turrets, rocket launchers) and rubber (heavy anti-air) when
  they reload, per GDD §9.3 (ammunition consumption). An empty store degrades defenses, it does not
  switch them off entirely.
- Repairs keep costing paper; upgrades may add ink or rubber at higher levels.
- Every cost is defined in `src/config.ts`, never inside gameplay classes, so balancing stays in
  one place.

### D. Raid losses and protected reserves

- Extend the raid loss rules in `server/world/Attacks.ts` (`loss`, `reward`) to all three
  resources: each resource has its own protected reserve and its own per-raid cap.
- The attacker's reward stays decoupled from the defender's loss (GDD §4.4, §8.4): the game pays
  the attacker, it does not transfer the stolen stock.
- The total loss from one raid stays capped; the repeat-raid factors (`PVP.repeat`,
  `PVP.farmWindow`), shields and cooldowns apply unchanged.
- The defense report (GDD §15.7) lists each resource lost or saved.
- Offline protection from v0.0.10 covers all three resources.

### E. Persistence and migration

- Add the new resources to the PostgreSQL schema with a versioned migration from the v0.0.10 to
  v0.0.14 save formats.
- Existing players start with sensible ink and rubber balances derived from their current base
  (for example, a share of storage capacity), so nobody loads into an empty store.
- Resource transactions stay idempotent: retried messages, refreshes, reconnects and restarts must
  not duplicate or lose any resource.
- The migration is tested against a copy of the real private alpha database before it runs on the
  live server, with a backup taken first and a documented rollback.

## 2. Upkeep and Anti-Snowball Controls

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Let success widen a player's strategic options without giving them endlessly compounding
advantages (GDD §13.5).

### A. Upkeep

- Introduce modest upkeep on high-level buildings and large defense networks, paid from production
  rather than stored resources, so a large base produces less net income than its raw numbers
  suggest.
- Upkeep never applies to the basic home, the first building in each slot category or anything a
  recovering player needs.
- Upkeep is shown plainly in base management (gross production, upkeep, net production).

### B. Caps and diminishing returns

- Keep storage caps per resource (GDD §4.3) and tune them against the new costs.
- Add diminishing returns on repeated sources: repeated identical missions, repeated kills of the
  same player (`PVP.killRepeat`) and repeated raids (`PVP.repeat`) keep their existing decay, and
  the same pattern is applied to any new source added in this version.
- Limit defense slots as in v0.0.7; do not add extra slots in this version.

### C. Catch-up for players who fall behind

- Boosted recovery below each protected reserve (the existing `boosted` income state) applies to
  all three resources.
- The mission director's recovery missions (v0.0.6, `src/director/MissionDirector.ts`) pay in the
  resource the player is short of.
- A player who loses everything above their reserve can still build a basic plane and fly a
  meaningful mission within a few minutes.

### D. What not to do

- Do not punish players simply for holding resources (GDD §11.5). Caps and upkeep slow growth,
  they do not confiscate.
- Do not introduce bounties or rivalry-based targeting here; that is v0.0.16.

## 3. Mission Content Expansion

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Give players more varied things to do between encounters, especially when the server
population is low (GDD §18, criterion 7).

### A. Stunts and exploration (GDD §7.2)

- Ring courses through the classroom geometry, building on the existing `rings` mission type in
  `src/missions/Mission.ts`.
- Low-altitude runs and maneuver challenges (tight turns, inverted passes, near-miss gates)
  measured by the server from the authoritative flight state.
- Discovery missions that send players to landmarks they have not visited yet.
- Rewards are modest; stunts are a fun detour, not a farming route.

### B. Timed challenges

- Time-trial versions of stunts and deliveries with personal and world records.
- Records are stored on the server (see section 5) and shown on the territory or landmark.

### C. Delivery variety

- Extend the `delivery` type: fragile cargo that fails on hard landings, cargo that slows the
  plane, deliveries to another player's territory (accepted only with their consent setting), and
  supply drops to AI outposts.
- Deliveries can pay ink or rubber so they become the natural way to top up a short resource.

### D. Multi-sortie operations in multiplayer

- Bring the single-player operations from `src/director/Operations.ts` into the multiplayer
  board: short chains of two or three sorties with a shared payout.
- Operations survive a disconnect between sorties (progress is persisted, per v0.0.10's rules
  for interrupted missions).
- Rewards for an operation are paid once, by the server, with idempotent completion.

### E. Mission generation

- New templates go into `src/director/Templates.ts` and `src/missions/MissionGenerator.ts` with
  eligibility checks and weights, following GDD §11.8 (explicit rules, no generative AI).
- The mission board keeps its safe, balanced and high-risk card structure.
- Every new mission type has a short-phrase name and icon for the v0.0.12 HUD.

## 4. Multiplayer World Events

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Make the shared world produce opportunities that bring players together, using the
single-player events from v0.0.6.

### A. Events to bring over

| Event | Source | Multiplayer behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| High-value shipment | `src/director/WorldEvents.ts` (`high-value`) | Any player may destroy it; reward split among contributors |
| Enemy convoy | `convoy` | AI Widewing crossing the map; any player may intercept |
| Supply run | `supply-run` | A friendly AI bomber heading to one player's home; others may help defend it |
| Supply drop | New | A crate of ink or rubber parachutes onto a neutral territory; first to land and collect it |
| Special event | New | Rare scheduled events (for example "ink storm": double ink rewards for 10 minutes) |

### B. Rules

- The server runs one event scheduler per world, paced by population and activity so a two-player
  world is not flooded.
- Contribution is measured with the same damage log used for kill credit (`creditFor` in
  `server/world/Attacks.ts`); rewards are split, never duplicated.
- Events never target an offline player's territory.
- Event bounties (`EVENTS.bounty` in `src/config.ts`) are tuned against the new economy.

### C. Presentation

- Events appear on the mini-map and as alerts using the v0.0.12 alert component, with v0.0.13
  audio cues.

## 5. Server-Side Progression

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Make medals, records, achievements, streaks and aircraft mastery part of the persistent
multiplayer profile, decided by the server.

### A. What moves

- Medals and mission records (v0.0.6, `src/progress/Progress.ts`).
- Achievements and streaks.
- Aircraft mastery per plane.
- The logbook history needed to display them.

### B. Rules

- The server awards progression from authoritative events only; clients display it.
- Progression is stored in PostgreSQL per player identity and survives restarts and reconnects.
- Single-player keeps its local save and local progression. Do not merge single-player and
  multiplayer progress in this version.
- Achievements that only make sense in single-player are not shown in multiplayer, and vice versa.
- Progression must never give a combat or economic advantage beyond what the GDD allows (mastery
  is recognition, not stats).

### C. Display

- The results screen, logbook and profile panel show multiplayer progression using the v0.0.12
  components.

## 6. Aircraft Roster Balance

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Make all eight aircraft meaningful choices under the new economy and the stealth system.

### A. Roles

| Aircraft | Inspiration | Role | Balance target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Swift | F-16 | Basic fighter, starter | Cheap, always reachable (free emergency version), solid all-round fighter, one small bomb |
| Falcon | Su-57 | Agile stealth fighter | Best turn rate; reduced radar signature; ink-hungry |
| Raptor | F-22 | Air superiority | Strongest dogfighter on energy; expensive; no bombs |
| Lightning | F-35 | Stealth multirole | Guns and a light rubber payload; lowest fighter signature; master of none |
| Dart | SR-71 | High-speed reconnaissance | Fastest plane; weak guns, no bombs; best recon bonuses |
| Thug | A-10 | Ground attack | Armoured, slow; strongest against structures and defenses; easy prey for fighters |
| Widewing | B-52 | Heavy bomber | Largest rubber payload; slow and vulnerable |
| Lancer | B-1B | Supersonic bomber | Fast with a dash; smaller payload than the Widewing |

### B. Requirements

- Tune speed, durability, payload, signature and costs (paper, ink, rubber) in `AIRCRAFT` in
  `src/config.ts` so no plane dominates every mission type (GDD §1.2, asymmetric warfare). The
  simulator (section 7) reports win rates and usage by plane.
- Mission suitability (`assessSuitability` in `src/aircraft/Aircraft.ts`) covers every new mission
  type and every plane, including ground-attack and stealth-sensitive missions.
- Kill rewards (`PVP.killShare` of plane cost) are rechecked so expensive planes are not farmable.
- The Swift stays the cheapest useful plane; no rebalance may make the starter useless.

## 7. Economy Simulation Tooling

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Measure balance across whole campaigns before players feel it.

### A. Campaign simulator

- Extend `src/director/Simulation.ts` from a single-player mission simulation to a multiplayer
  campaign simulation: several simulated players with different profiles (newcomer, steady,
  aggressive, idle, exploiter) over days of game time.
- Simulate production, upkeep, missions, raids, events, losses and offline time with the real
  rule classes, not a copy of them.

### B. Reports

- Resource balances over time per profile, the gap between leader and last, time to recover after
  a lost raid, and resource flows by source and sink (GDD §13.3, §13.4).
- Flags for runaway leaders, dead ends and any strategy whose expected value beats normal play
  (for example, abandoning missions or trading raids between accounts).

### C. Developer access

- Runnable from the command line (`npm run sim -- ...`) and in CI with a short fixed seed.
- Results can be exported as CSV or JSON for the developer to inspect.

## 8. Radar Signature and Stealth System

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Give stealth aircraft a real, readable advantage in detection without ever making them
invisible or unbeatable.

### A. Signature model

- Each aircraft gets a radar signature value in `AIRCRAFT` (`src/config.ts`): normal for most, low
  for the Falcon and Lightning (and the Ghost in v0.0.16).
- Signature scales the **detection range** of radar stations, turrets and rocket launchers
  (`src/defenses/`), of AI pilots' target acquisition (`src/ai/`) and of the threat panel's
  "spotted" logic. A low-signature plane is spotted closer, not never.
- Signature rises temporarily when the plane fires, opens its bomb bay or boosts, so attacking
  reveals the attacker.
- Rocket lock-on takes longer against low-signature planes, with a floor so locks remain possible.
- Visual detection is unchanged: players and AI always see a plane within visual range.

### B. Readability and fairness

- The stealth plane's pilot sees its current detection state ("hidden", "detected", "locked") in
  the v0.0.12 HUD; defenders see a "contact" marker when detected, with the same icons as today.
- Raid warnings for defenders still fire, at the reduced detection range; the new-player
  protection, offline protection and raid rules in `PVP` are unchanged.
- No permanent invisibility, no immunity to guns, no stealth stacking from escorts or cosmetics.

### C. Server authority

- Detection and lock decisions are made on the server for multiplayer (`server/world/`), with the
  client only displaying them; clients do not receive positions they should not know about where
  that matters for fairness (prepares v0.0.17's interest-based updates).

### D. Tests

- Unit tests for detection range by signature, the fire and bomb-bay penalties and the lock floor.
- Defense and AI tests: a Lightning is detected later than a Swift on the same path, and still
  detected.
- A two-player test where a stealth attacker raids and the defender still gets a warning in time to
  launch an interceptor.

## 9. New Aircraft: Lightning and Thug

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Add the Lightning and the Thug, bringing the roster to eight, each filling a role the
roster lacks.

### A. Roles and handling

| Aircraft | Inspiration | Role | Visual character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lightning | F-35 | Stealth multirole | Compact faceted body, single large engine, trapezoid wings, internal bay |
| Thug | A-10 | Ground attack | Straight thick wings, twin tail fins, two engines high on the rear body, oversized nose gun |

- **Lightning:** the GDD's multipurpose role (§5.2): guns and a light rubber payload carried
  internally, the lowest fighter signature, average speed and agility. Good at everything, the
  best at nothing; its signature rises when the bay opens.
- **Thug:** slow, very durable (armoured), a heavy ink cannon that damages structures and
  defenses strongly, light rubber bombs, low-altitude stability. Poor top speed and climb make it
  an easy target for fighters, so it wants escorts or a quiet sky.

### B. Model requirements

Reuses the aircraft model checklist from v0.0.11:

- Distinctive geometry that reads at speed and at distance, and a stylised folded-paper
  interpretation of the real aircraft, not a replica.
- Visible paper folds and creases.
- Low-poly optimisation within the agreed aircraft triangle budget.
- Consistent outline rendering (aircraft tier, boldest lines).
- Correct collision geometry, shared by client and server.
- Weapon and payload attachment points.
- Folding and construction animation (`src/aircraft/PlaneModel.ts`, `PlaneShapes.ts`).
- Multiplayer synchronisation, including payload and animation state on remote planes.
- Damage and destruction effects (crumple and tear).
- Controller handling tuned with the v0.0.11 input-action layer and analog curves.
- Lightning: an opening internal bay synchronised with release.
- Thug: a visible nose cannon with muzzle effects, and damage visuals that show armour holding
  (dents and tears before crumpling).

### C. Ground-attack missions

- New mission templates built for the Thug and suited to the Lightning and Swift: strafe a
  defense line, destroy a row of exposed structures, suppress a fortified territory's turrets
  under time pressure, protect a supply run by clearing ground defenses.
- Use the existing destructibles and defense targets (`src/combat/Destructibles.ts`,
  `src/defenses/`), and the new resource costs and rewards from section 1.
- Player territories are hit only through raid missions, as in v0.0.9; ground-attack planes
  follow the same raid caps, cooldowns and protections.

### D. Balance, cost and mission fit

- Lightning: mid-to-high cost in paper and ink, plus rubber for its payload.
- Thug: mid cost, high ink use; best value against defenses, poor value in dogfights.
- Both added to `AircraftId`, `AIRCRAFT_ORDER`, AI roles, the mission director's suitability
  checks and server validation; persisted records accept the new ids.

### E. Interface and audio integration

- Garage cards and inspector entries, the Lightning's stealth state in the HUD, the Thug's armour
  in the health bar, all with v0.0.12 components.
- Engine sounds, the Thug's cannon and the Lightning's bay in the v0.0.13 audio system.

### F. Tests

- Flight model, weapons, costs, signature, armour, mission fit and server validation tests.
- Ground-attack mission tests (completion, failure, rewards, no farming).
- A two-player session: one player in a Thug attacking defenses, the other intercepting in a
  Raptor or Falcon.

**Priority:** Critical

### A. Automated tests

- Unit tests for per-resource production, capacity, reserves, upkeep and raid losses.
- Migration tests from every persisted save version since v0.0.10.
- Idempotency tests: duplicate mission completion, retried rearm, reconnect mid-transaction,
  server restart mid-transaction.
- Server tests for multiplayer events, contribution splits and operations across disconnects.
- Simulation tests in CI asserting no runaway leader and no unrecoverable player in a fixed-seed
  campaign.
- All earlier tests keep passing.

### B. Browser and bot testing

- Bots (`server/bots/`) exercise the new costs, rearming and events.
- Headless browser runs check the new HUD values and panels.

### C. Two-player playtest

- The developer and the invited friend play at least two sessions on the private server and
  record impressions in the feedback log: is each resource meaningful, is anyone stuck, does a
  wealthy player pull away.

- [ ] Paper, ink and rubber balls are produced, stored, spent and lost by server-authoritative rules.
- [ ] Each resource has a protected reserve and a per-raid loss cap.
- [ ] Aircraft, rearming, defenses and repairs use the new costs; the basic plane stays reachable.
- [ ] Persisted worlds and players from v0.0.10 onward migrate without loss.
- [ ] No resource can be duplicated by retries, refreshes, reconnects or restarts.
- [ ] Upkeep and caps are in place and visible in base management.
- [ ] Stunts, timed challenges, varied deliveries and multiplayer operations are playable.
- [ ] Multiplayer world events run, split rewards fairly and never target offline players.
- [ ] Medals, records, achievements and mastery are stored and awarded by the server.
- [ ] All eight aircraft are meaningful choices; the Swift stays the reachable starter.
- [ ] The radar signature system works for defenses, AI and players, is readable, and never makes
      a plane invisible; the Falcon's stealth trait is active.
- [ ] The Lightning and the Thug are playable with custom models, and ground-attack missions work.
- [ ] The campaign simulator runs in CI and shows no runaway leader and no dead end.
- [ ] All earlier automated tests and the new tests pass.
- [ ] The developer and invited friend have played the new economy and their feedback is addressed.
- [ ] The README and GDD describe the new economy accurately.

## Final design principle

More resources must mean more interesting choices, not more numbers to watch. A player who
falls behind can always fly again, a player who gets ahead gains options rather than a permanent
lead, and every resource a player spends or loses is decided fairly by the server.
