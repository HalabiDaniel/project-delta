# paperwars.io: Version 0.0.19 Development Plan

**Development stage:** Closed alpha (preparing for public access)
**Release type:** Infrastructure, operations and compliance milestone
**Release theme:** Accounts, Matchmaking, Campaigns, Moderation and a Production Foundation
**Builds on:** [v0.0.18](v0.0.18.md) (Customization and social)
**Followed by:** [v0.0.20](v0.0.20.md) (Release candidate)

Part of the [roadmap to v0.1.0](README.md).

---

## Overview

Up to version 0.0.18 paperwars.io has been played by the developer, an invited friend and a small invited group. They are known, trusted and few. The private identity and invite system from v0.0.10, the single persistent world, and the manual deployment are enough for them.

Version 0.1.0 opens the game to strangers. Strangers need real accounts, a way to find a world, worlds that start and end, protection from abuse, a clear legal basis for handling their data, and a server setup that keeps working when one machine fails.

Version 0.0.19 builds all of that. It is mostly invisible to players when it works, which is the point.

The release has eight central objectives:

1. Replace private identities with public accounts: email or OAuth sign-in, recovery, guest upgrade and account deletion.
2. Add matchmaking that places players into suitable worlds and creates new worlds automatically.
3. Implement the campaign lifecycle from GDD §3.3: worlds of about seven days, a reset flow, end-of-campaign rewards and clear carry-over rules.
4. Implement inactive-territory rules from GDD §3.4.
5. Provide moderation: a name filter, a report queue, admin tools and an audit log.
6. Detect and limit abuse: multiple accounts, reward farming and cheating clients.
7. Add privacy-light analytics and crash reporting, and the legal basis for them (privacy policy, terms of service, GDPR compliance).
8. Move to production infrastructure: several game servers, staging, automated deploys, backups with tested restores, monitoring, and load tests.

The release must preserve the PlayCanvas client, the TypeScript game rules shared between client and server, the Node and Colyseus server, and PostgreSQL. The server stays authoritative for everything that matters (GDD §16.2).

Do not add new gameplay systems in this version. Balance changes are allowed only where campaigns or matchmaking require them.

## 1. Public Accounts

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Let anyone create an account, keep it secure, recover it, and delete it.

### A. Sign-in methods

| Method | Notes |
|---|---|
| Guest | Immediate play, no details required; progress tied to a device token |
| Email | Passwordless magic link or email plus password with secure hashing (Argon2id or bcrypt) |
| OAuth | Google and Discord to start; others later |

- Guests must be able to play within about a minute (GDD §18, criterion 1).
- A guest can upgrade to an email or OAuth account at any time and keeps all progress, inventory and territory.
- One account may link several sign-in methods.

### B. Sessions and security

- Short-lived access tokens and rotating refresh tokens in secure, HTTP-only cookies.
- The Colyseus join flow verifies the token server-side; the client never chooses its own player ID.
- Rate limits on sign-in, sign-up, magic-link and recovery endpoints.
- Email verification before an account can send friend requests or reports.
- Secrets in environment variables or a secret manager, never in the repository.

### C. Recovery

- Email-based recovery for email accounts.
- OAuth accounts recover through their provider.
- Guest accounts are warned that clearing browser data loses progress, and prompted to upgrade after their first meaningful progress.

### D. Display names

- Display names are unique per world (not globally), filtered (section 5) and changeable with a cooldown.
- Ownership and identity always use the account ID, never the display name.

### E. Account deletion and data export

- Players can delete their account from settings. Deletion removes personal data, anonymises historic records needed for other players' battle reports, and frees their territory.
- Players can export their personal data (GDPR right of access).
- Deletion and export requests are logged and completed within the legally required time.

### F. Migration from the private alpha

- Private alpha identities from v0.0.10 are migrated to accounts by linking an email or OAuth sign-in.
- Migration is tested on a copy of the real alpha database before it runs.

## 2. Matchmaking and World Management

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Put every player into a world where they can have a good time, without waiting.

### A. World directory

- The server keeps a registry of active worlds: region, environment (v0.0.17), campaign day, player count, territory availability and new-player friendliness.
- The lobby from v0.0.14 shows recommended worlds first, with an option to browse.

### B. Placement rules

Matchmaking scores candidate worlds by:

- Free territory slots.
- Region and measured latency.
- Campaign day (new players are placed in early-campaign worlds where possible).
- Strength balance (avoid placing a new player into a world dominated by veterans).
- Friends and squad members already in the world (with the squad limit from v0.0.18).

### C. Automatic world creation

- When recommended worlds fill up, the system creates a new world on a server with spare capacity.
- Worlds with too few players for too long are offered a merge or wind down at the end of their campaign; players are never moved mid-campaign without consent.
- Minimum population is supported by AI aircraft and bots (GDD §18, criterion 7: engaging activities at low population).

### D. Rejoining

- A player with a territory in an active world always rejoins that world first.
- A player can hold a territory in only one world per campaign at a time.

## 3. Campaign Lifecycle

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Give worlds a beginning, a middle and an end, so wealth does not accumulate forever and latecomers can compete (GDD §3.3).

### A. Campaign length and phases

| Phase | Duration (provisional) | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Day 1 | Extra new-player protection, no raids for the first hours |
| Main | Days 2 to 6 | Normal rules |
| Final day | Day 7 | Campaign standings visible, bounties and events intensify |
| Results | About 1 hour | Standings frozen, results shown, rewards granted |
| Reset | Immediate | World archived, a fresh world opens |

All durations are configuration, not code, and are tuned in the v0.1.0 test rounds.

### B. What resets and what carries over

| Resets with the world | Carries over on the account |
|---|---|
| Territory ownership | Cosmetic inventory and loadouts (v0.0.18) |
| Buildings and their levels | Achievements and mastery |
| Stored resources | Lifetime statistics |
| Active missions, bounties, rivalries | Friends, squads, settings |
| Campaign reputation | Preferred aircraft and saved loadouts |

### C. End-of-campaign rewards

- Placements and titles (top defender, top raider, most missions, best newcomer).
- Cosmetic rewards only; no resources carry into the next campaign.
- Rewards are granted idempotently by the server when results are frozen.

### D. Reset flow

- Players get notices at 24 hours, 1 hour and 10 minutes before the end.
- At reset the world state is archived for statistics and support, then removed from active storage.
- Players are offered the next world of the same region and environment, with friends and squad kept together where possible.

## 4. Inactive Territories

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Stop absent players from holding valuable slots, without punishing short absences (GDD §3.4).

### A. Rules (provisional)

| Absence | Effect |
|---|---|
| Up to 24 hours | Territory fully protected (offline protection from v0.0.10) |
| 24 to 72 hours | Territory marked inactive; still protected; production paused at storage cap |
| Over 72 hours | Territory released; the player keeps their account rewards and can join again |

- Players see these rules in the lobby and in their territory panel.
- Players are notified before their territory is released, if they have a verified email and have opted in.

### B. Interaction with campaigns

- A released player can rejoin the same world if a slot is free, as a new territory with starting resources.
- Released territories become available to new players.

## 5. Moderation

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Give the developer the tools to keep the game friendly once strangers arrive (GDD §12.4).

### A. Name and banner filter

- Display names, squad names and banner text are checked against a block list and pattern rules (including common letter substitutions) when set.
- Flagged names are rejected with a clear reason, or sent for review when the case is unclear.

### B. Reports

- Players can report another player from the scoreboard, kill feed, recent players or friends list.
- Report reasons: offensive name, cheating, harassment or targeting, exploit abuse, other.
- Reports include automatic context (world, time, recent encounters between the two players).
- Duplicate reports are grouped. Reporters are rate-limited.

### C. Admin tools

A protected admin console with:

- Report queue with filtering and status (open, actioned, dismissed).
- Player lookup: accounts, sign-in methods, worlds, recent activity, flags.
- Actions: warn, rename, mute emotes, kick, temporary ban, permanent ban, revoke items, restore items.
- World tools: inspect a world, pause it, end it early with notice, move a player with their consent.

### D. Audit log

- Every admin action is recorded with the admin, time, target, reason and before and after state.
- The audit log is append-only and kept separately from game data.

### E. Player protection

- Mute and block (v0.0.16 and v0.0.18) are always available and work immediately.
- The mission director never offers missions that repeatedly target the same player against their will; cooldowns from v0.0.9 still apply.

## 6. Abuse Prevention

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Make cheating, farming and multiple accounts unprofitable and detectable (GDD §13.6).

### A. Multiple accounts

- Signals: device tokens, IP address ranges, timing patterns and repeated interactions.
- Accounts suspected of being linked cannot earn rewards from each other and cannot occupy several territories in one world.
- Signals are for detection and review, not automatic permanent bans.

### B. Reward farming

- Existing rules (decaying raid rewards, assist and participation thresholds, bounty rules from v0.0.9 and v0.0.16, squad rules from v0.0.18) are reviewed together.
- New server checks flag accounts whose reward rate is far above normal, or who repeatedly lose to the same opponent.

### C. Cheating clients

- The server already decides positions, hits and rewards. Add validation for input frames that imply impossible turn rates, speeds or fire rates.
- Repeated violations are logged, flagged and, past a threshold, disconnect the player.
- Never trust the client for damage, rewards, ownership or mission success.

### D. Network abuse

- Rate limits per connection for messages and commands.
- Message size limits and schema validation for every message in `src/net/protocol.ts`.
- Limits on concurrent connections per account and per IP.

## 7. Analytics and Crash Reporting

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Learn whether the game works for players, while collecting as little data as possible.

### A. Principles

- Collect only what answers a specific question.
- No third-party advertising trackers.
- Event data uses the account ID or a pseudonymous ID, never names or emails.
- Respect consent where required; analytics that need consent are off until given.

### B. Events

| Area | Examples |
|---|---|
| Funnel | Page loaded, first flight, first mission complete, first return visit |
| Retention | Day 1, day 7 and day 30 returns, session length |
| Gameplay | Mission offers and choices, results, aircraft picked, causes of loss |
| Economy | Resource sources and sinks per campaign day |
| Technical | Load time, FPS buckets, disconnects, errors |

### C. Crash and error reporting

- Client errors and unhandled promise rejections are captured with the build version and browser.
- Server errors are captured with world and room context.
- Personal data is scrubbed before sending.

### D. Dashboards

- A small dashboard covering the success criteria in GDD §18 that can be measured.
- Used in v0.1.0's test rounds.

## 8. Legal and Compliance

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Have a clear legal basis for running a public online game.

### A. Documents

- Privacy policy: what is collected, why, how long it is kept, who processes it, how to delete or export data.
- Terms of service: acceptable use, account termination, virtual items having no cash value, liability.
- Cookie and storage notice where required.
- Licence attributions (already in the credits from v0.0.14).

### B. GDPR

- Lawful basis for each type of data.
- A record of processors (hosting, email, analytics, crash reporting, OAuth providers).
- Data retention periods enforced by scheduled jobs.
- Right of access, rectification, erasure and portability (section 1.E).

### C. Age

- Decide the minimum age and how it is communicated (the developer decides with legal advice).

### D. Review

- The developer approves all legal texts. The agent drafts structure and technical facts, not legal advice.

## 9. Production Infrastructure

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Run the game reliably for many players across several machines.

### A. Environments

| Environment | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Local | Development (`npm run dev`, `npm run server`) |
| Staging | Same setup as production, used for every release before it goes live |
| Production | Public worlds |

### B. Game servers

- Several Colyseus server processes, each hosting several worlds.
- A matchmaker or presence service (Colyseus presence with Redis, or equivalent) that knows which process hosts which world.
- Worlds can be drained from a server for maintenance without ending them abruptly.
- Regional deployments where v0.0.17's region support requires them.

### C. Deploys

- Automated build, test and deploy pipeline on every merge to the release branch.
- Database migrations run automatically and are reversible or have a tested rollback plan.
- Client and server versions are checked at connect; mismatches show the v0.0.14 version-mismatch screen.

### D. Backups and restore drills

- Automated daily full backups and continuous write-ahead log archiving for PostgreSQL.
- Backups stored separately from the primary database.
- A restore drill is performed and documented before release: restore to staging, verify a world and accounts.

### E. Monitoring and alerts

- Health checks for game servers, the database and the matchmaker.
- Metrics: players online, worlds, tick time, message rate, memory, CPU, database latency, error rate.
- Alerts to the developer for outages, error spikes and backups failing.

### F. Operations documentation

- Runbooks for: deploy, rollback, restore from backup, drain a server, end a world early, rotate secrets, respond to an outage.

---

## 10. Load Testing

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Know how many players and worlds the system can handle before strangers arrive.

### A. Tooling

- Extend `server/loadtest.ts` and the bots in `server/bots/` to run many worlds and many bots at once against staging.
- Include sign-in, matchmaking, joining, flying, combat, missions, raids and disconnects.

### B. Targets (to confirm with the developer)

- A defined number of concurrent worlds per server process at full population.
- Server tick time stays within budget at that load.
- Matchmaking places a player in under a few seconds.
- No data loss under a simulated server crash.

### C. Results

- Results are published in the README's measurements section, including hardware and settings.

---

## 11. Testing and Verification

- Automated tests for account flows, token checks, guest upgrade, deletion and export.
- Automated tests for matchmaking scoring, world creation and the squad limit.
- A compressed campaign (minutes instead of days) runs end to end: opening, main, final day, results, reset, carry-over.
- Inactive territory rules tested with simulated clocks.
- Moderation and admin actions tested, including the audit log.
- Abuse checks tested with bots that cheat on purpose (impossible turns, fire rate, farming patterns).
- Restore drill completed on staging.
- Load tests completed and recorded.
- Invited group playtest of the full public flow: sign up, matchmaking, play, campaign end.

## 15. Definition of Done

- [ ] Guest, email and OAuth sign-in work; guests can upgrade without losing progress.
- [ ] Recovery, deletion and data export work.
- [ ] Private alpha identities have been migrated.
- [ ] Matchmaking places players into suitable worlds and creates new worlds automatically.
- [ ] A full campaign runs from opening to reset, with correct carry-over and rewards (verified in compressed time).
- [ ] Inactive territory rules work and are shown to players.
- [ ] Name filter, reports, admin console and audit log work.
- [ ] Abuse prevention detects linked accounts, farming and impossible client input.
- [ ] Analytics and crash reporting work and respect consent.
- [ ] Privacy policy and terms of service are approved and published.
- [ ] Staging and production run on several server processes with automated deploys.
- [ ] Backups run and a restore drill has been completed and documented.
- [ ] Monitoring and alerts reach the developer.
- [ ] Load tests meet the agreed targets.
- [ ] All earlier automated tests pass.

---

## Final design principle

A stranger should be able to arrive, play, and come back next week to find their progress safe and the world fair. If a server fails, nobody should lose anything that matters. If someone behaves badly, the developer should be able to see it and act on it.
