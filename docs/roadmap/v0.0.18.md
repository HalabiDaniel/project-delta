# paperwars.io: Version 0.0.18 Development Plan

**Development stage:** Private alpha (expanding invited group)
**Release type:** Feature milestone
**Release theme:** Cosmetic Customization, Earned Cosmetics and Social Foundations
**Builds on:** [v0.0.17](v0.0.17.md) (World scale and environments)
**Followed by:** [v0.0.19](v0.0.19.md) (Public readiness)

Part of the [roadmap to v0.1.0](README.md).

---

## Overview

By version 0.0.17 paperwars.io has a persistent, server-authoritative multiplayer world (v0.0.10), its full art and controller support (v0.0.11), a rebuilt HUD (v0.0.12), audio (v0.0.13), menus and branding (v0.0.14), a deeper economy (v0.0.15), advanced multiplayer systems such as escorts, bounties and rivalries (v0.0.16), and worlds of 12 to 16 territories across several classroom-scale environments (v0.0.17).

What players still cannot do is make the game their own. Every Falcon looks like every other Falcon, every home desk is dressed the same way, and the only relationship between two players is the one the mission director creates for them.

Version 0.0.18 adds the first layer of personal expression and social structure. It answers GDD §5.6 (aircraft customization), the non-paid half of §14 (cosmetic items), and the cooperation and communication parts of §12.

The release has five central objectives:

1. Give players cosmetic-only customization for their aircraft: paper colours, printed patterns, stickers, trails and folding variations, visible to every other player.
2. Let players decorate their home territory with themes that never affect gameplay.
3. Introduce earned cosmetics: rewards from achievements, campaign results and aircraft mastery.
4. Build a server-side cosmetic inventory that will later become the foundation of the cosmetic shop (v0.0.20 infrastructure, enabled after v0.1.0).
5. Add the first social systems: a friends list, invites, recent players, and small capped alliances (squads) that cannot be used to farm rewards.

The release must preserve the existing PlayCanvas, TypeScript, Node and Colyseus architecture, the persistence layer from v0.0.10, and the design system from v0.0.12.

**Cosmetics must never affect competitive performance.** No cosmetic may change an aircraft's speed, turn rate, durability, hitbox, silhouette readability, ammunition, payload or detection profile. This rule is absolute and is enforced by tests.

No purchasing is introduced in this version. Everything added is earned through play.

## 1. Aircraft Customization

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Let every player give their aircraft a recognisable personal look, while keeping every aircraft equally readable in combat.

### A. Customization slots

Each aircraft pattern (Dart, Falcon, Widewing, Shadow, Courier, and any additions from v0.0.15) gets the same set of cosmetic slots:

| Slot | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Paper colour | White, sky blue, sticky-note yellow, mint, coral, kraft brown | Base sheet colour, applied through the shared paper material |
| Printed pattern | Notebook lines, graph grid, newspaper, polka dots, stripes, origami prints | Procedural or small atlas textures, tinted by the paper colour |
| Stickers | Stars, lightning bolts, smiley faces, numbers, team marks | Up to a fixed number per aircraft (start with 3), on predefined anchor points |
| Trail | Pencil smudge, ink drip, confetti, glitter, eraser dust | Replaces the default flight trail from `src/aircraft/FlightTrails.ts` |
| Folding variation | Wing-tip curls, nose fold styles, tail flap shapes | Purely visual geometry variants that keep the same silhouette envelope and collision |

Customization is stored per aircraft pattern, so a player's Falcon and Widewing can look different.

### B. Readability rules

Ownership and threat colours from the art direction (blue friendly, red hostile, grey neutral, orange objective) must remain dominant over personal cosmetics.

- Ownership is shown by a separate, always-on marker and outline tint that cosmetics cannot override.
- Paper colours that would be confused with ownership colours at distance are desaturated or excluded from the palette.
- Stickers and patterns fade with distance, using the same distance falloff as pencil outlines.
- Trails have a maximum length, opacity and particle count, and cannot hide the aircraft or its projectiles.
- Folding variations must stay inside the base aircraft's silhouette envelope (checked by an automated bounding test).

### C. Customization interface

Add a customization view to the garage introduced in v0.0.12:

- A 3D preview reusing `src/aircraft/HangarPreview.ts`, rotatable with mouse and right stick.
- One row per slot, with items shown as swatches or thumbnails.
- Locked items shown with how to earn them (achievement name, campaign result, mastery level).
- Randomise, reset to default, and copy from another aircraft.
- Full controller navigation with visible focus, following the v0.0.12 design system.
- Changes apply only when the player confirms. Customization cannot be changed while airborne.

### D. Multiplayer synchronization

- Each player's equipped cosmetics are part of their persistent profile, not of each sortie.
- On launch, the server includes a compact cosmetic descriptor (item IDs, not textures) in the aircraft state in `src/net/schema.ts`.
- Clients resolve the descriptor locally against the cosmetic catalogue.
- Unknown item IDs (for example, an older client) fall back to the default look without errors.
- The server validates that the player owns every item in the descriptor before accepting it.

### E. Performance

- Cosmetics reuse the shared material library and add no new shaders.
- Patterns and sticker images live in a small number of texture atlases.
- An 8-player dogfight with every player fully customised must stay within the v0.0.17 performance targets.
- A graphics setting can reduce other players' trails and stickers to defaults on low-end hardware.

## 2. Territory Decoration Themes

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Let players make their home territory feel like theirs without changing how it plays.

### A. Decoration themes

A decoration theme restyles the home territory's surface, trims and decorative props:

- Examples: Sticker Collector, Doodle Margins, Crayon Box, Science Fair, Origami Garden, Night Study.
- Themes apply on top of the territory's environment theme from v0.0.11 and v0.0.17.
- They change materials, decorative props and banner art only.

### B. Gameplay invariants

Decoration must not change:

- Construction slot positions, sizes or count.
- Building health, cost or behaviour.
- Collision geometry, landing strips, take-off paths or launch pads.
- Line of sight for turrets and radar.
- Visibility of incoming bombers or of the territory to attackers.

Automated tests extend the existing "nothing sits on the runway, take-off path, slots or store" checks from v0.0.8 to every decoration theme.

### C. Banner

- Each player gets a territory banner: shape, colour and one emblem.
- The banner is shown on their territory, on the scoreboard and in kill-feed entries.
- Banners are where GDD §12.4's "rival banners" and v0.0.16's ink marking mischief land.

### D. Synchronization and persistence

- The equipped theme and banner are stored with the territory in the database.
- When a campaign resets (v0.0.19), the player's decoration choices carry over to their new territory.

## 3. Earned Cosmetics

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Give players reasons to keep playing that reward skill and commitment, not spending.

### A. Sources

| Source | Examples |
|---|---|
| Achievements | "First interception", "Defend 10 raids", "Win a dogfight against a Shadow in a Dart" |
| Aircraft mastery | Mastery levels per aircraft (from v0.0.6, moved to the server in v0.0.15) unlock patterns and folding variations |
| Campaign results | End-of-campaign placement, best defender, most missions; awarded when campaigns exist (v0.0.19), defined now |
| Milestones | Total sorties, total missions, days played |
| Events | LiveOps events (v0.0.20) can grant time-limited cosmetics |

### B. Fairness rules

- Every earned cosmetic is reachable by a normal player within a reasonable time; a few prestige items can be harder.
- No cosmetic is tied to winning against specific players, to avoid targeted farming.
- Achievements that involve other players require meaningful participation (the v0.0.9 assist and participation rules apply).
- Achievements cannot be earned in private worlds with only alliance members or the same people repeatedly beyond a cap.

### C. Unlock presentation

- A short unlock card after the mission result, using the v0.0.12 result screen.
- A collection screen showing owned, locked and hidden items, with progress towards each.
- Unlocks are announced once and never repeated.

### D. Server authority

- All unlock checks run on the server, from the server's mission and combat records.
- Grants are idempotent: an unlock processed twice (retry, reconnect, restart) grants the item once.

## 4. Cosmetic Inventory System

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Store cosmetic ownership durably and in a form that the later shop can reuse.

### A. Data model

Extend the PostgreSQL schema from v0.0.10:

| Table | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `cosmetic_items` | Catalogue: ID, slot, rarity, source, availability window, version |
| `player_inventory` | Which player owns which item, when, from which source (earned, event, admin, future purchase) |
| `player_loadouts` | Equipped items per aircraft pattern, territory theme and banner |
| `inventory_ledger` | Append-only record of every grant and removal, with a reason and idempotency key |

### B. Catalogue

- The catalogue is data, not code: a versioned file in the repository, loaded into the database by migration.
- Items can be retired (no longer earnable) without removing them from owners.
- Every item has a source field so the future shop can distinguish earned items from purchasable ones.

### C. Rules

- Ownership changes only through server-side grants with an idempotency key.
- The client never asserts ownership; it only requests to equip.
- Admin grants and removals (for testing and support) are logged with the admin's identity.
- Inventory survives campaign resets.

### D. Groundwork for the shop

- No prices, currency or payment code in this version.
- The data model leaves room for a `purchase` source and a receipt reference, used by the disabled shop infrastructure in v0.0.20.

## 5. Friends, Invites and Recent Players

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Make it easy to play with the people you like.

### A. Friends list

- Send, accept, decline and remove friend requests by player ID or invite link.
- Show each friend's online status and which world they are in.
- Join a friend's world when it has a free seat and the player is eligible.
- Block a player: hides their requests and invites and hides them from recent players.

### B. Invites

- Invite links or short codes for a specific world (building on the invite code from v0.0.10 and the lobby from v0.0.14).
- Invites expire and can be revoked.
- Joining by invite still goes through the normal territory claim and new-player protection.

### C. Recent players

- A list of players met recently (same world, combat, cooperation, raids), with what happened.
- One-click friend request, mute or report from the list.
- Built on the encounter records from v0.0.16's rivalry system.

### D. Privacy

- Players can set who may send friend requests (everyone, recent players only, nobody).
- Online status can be hidden.
- No free-text chat is introduced in this version (emotes only, as in v0.0.9 and v0.0.16).

## 6. Alliances (Squads)

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Let small groups cooperate formally (GDD §12.2) without letting them dominate a world or farm each other.

### A. Structure

- A squad has a name, a banner and a small member cap (start with 3, provisional).
- Members in the same world are friendly: no friendly fire, shared radar contacts, and the ability to escort and defend each other.
- A player can belong to one squad at a time. Leaving has a cooldown.

### B. World limits

- Each world limits how many members of one squad can hold territories (for example, no more than a quarter of the slots).
- Matchmaking (v0.0.19) will respect this limit; for now it applies to joining and invites.
- The mission director treats a squad as a group when judging relative strength, so bounties and challenge missions scale against squads.

### C. Anti-farming

- Squad members cannot raid each other's territories.
- No rewards for kills, assists or interceptions between current or recent squad members (with a cooldown after leaving).
- Repeated join-leave cycles are rate-limited.
- Server-side checks flag suspicious patterns: repeated losses to the same player, matched entry and exit times, unusual reward rates. Flags go to the moderation tools from v0.0.19.

### D. Squad features

- A squad roster with online status.
- Squad defense alerts: members are told when another member's territory is raided.
- Squad emotes and banner on the scoreboard.

## 7. Replays and Spectating (Optional)

**Priority:** Medium (may be deferred)
**Goal:** Let players watch notable fights and share them, if it can be done cheaply.

### A. Spectating

- A friend can spectate a world they are not playing in, with a delay (for example, 30 seconds) so it cannot be used for scouting.
- Spectators use the chase camera or a free camera, and see no private data.

### B. Short replays

- The server already keeps snapshot history for lag compensation (`server/world/History.ts`). A short replay of the last minute of a notable event (shot down a bounty target, defended a raid) can be built from recorded snapshots.
- Replays are watched in-game. Exporting video is out of scope.

### C. Decision point

If either feature threatens the schedule or server performance, defer it to a 0.1.x improvement release and record the decision.

## 8. Testing and Verification

### A. Automated tests

- Cosmetic descriptors do not change any flight model, damage, collision or detection value (one test per aircraft per slot).
- Folding variations stay inside the base silhouette envelope.
- Decoration themes never obstruct slots, runways, take-off paths or turret lines of sight.
- Grants are idempotent across retries, reconnects and restarts.
- The client cannot equip items it does not own.
- Squad rules: no friendly fire, no raids, no rewards between members or recent members.
- Friend, invite and block flows, including expired and revoked invites.

### B. Multiplayer tests

- Bots with random cosmetics in an 8-player and a 16-territory world: all clients see the same looks.
- An older client receiving an unknown item ID falls back to defaults.
- Performance comparison with and without full cosmetics.

### C. Playtests

- The developer, the friend and the invited group customise their aircraft and territories, form a squad and play a session.
- Questions: Can you tell friend from foe instantly? Is anything unfair? Do the earned cosmetics feel worth chasing?

## 9. Release Scope and Boundaries

Defer:

| Deferred item | Owning version |
|---|---|
| Public accounts, matchmaking, campaigns | v0.0.19 |
| Moderation queue and admin tools | v0.0.19 |
| Shop infrastructure (disabled) | v0.0.20 |
| Enabling purchases | 0.2.0 or later, after retention is proven |
| Free-text chat | Not planned before 1.0.0; requires moderation review |
| Large clans, clan wars, clan territories | After 1.0.0, if at all |
| Video export of replays | After v0.1.0 |

Existing systems from earlier versions must keep working.

## 10. Developer Responsibilities

- Approve the cosmetic palette, the patterns and the sticker set (or supply art).
- Approve the readability rules and the ownership-colour exclusions.
- Decide the squad size cap and per-world squad limit for testing.
- Decide whether replays and spectating are in or deferred.
- Playtest with the invited group and report what feels unfair or unreadable.

- Preserve every working feature from earlier versions.
- Record decisions and open questions in the README.

## 12. Definition of Done

- [ ] All aircraft support paper colour, pattern, stickers, trail and folding variation.
- [ ] Cosmetics are visible and identical for every player in the world.
- [ ] Automated tests prove cosmetics have no gameplay effect.
- [ ] Ownership and threat colours remain readable with any combination of cosmetics.
- [ ] Territory decoration themes and banners work and never obstruct gameplay.
- [ ] Earned cosmetics unlock from achievements, mastery and milestones, server-side and idempotently.
- [ ] The cosmetic inventory and ledger persist and survive restarts and campaign resets.
- [ ] Friends, invites, recent players and blocking work.
- [ ] Squads work with their caps, and anti-farming rules are enforced and tested.
- [ ] Replays and spectating are either working or explicitly deferred.
- [ ] Performance stays within the v0.0.17 targets with full cosmetics.
- [ ] All earlier automated tests pass.
- [ ] The invited group has played a session with customization and squads.

---

## Final design principle

Players should be able to recognise their own plane, their friends' planes and their own desk at a glance, and feel proud of them, without anyone ever being able to buy, earn or equip an advantage. Social systems should make it easier to play together, never easier to gang up or farm.
