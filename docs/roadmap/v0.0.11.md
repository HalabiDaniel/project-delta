# paperwars.io: Version 0.0.11 Development Plan

**Development stage:** Private two-player alpha (presentation and input)
**Release type:** Major art integration and input milestone
**Release theme:** Complete Art Integration, Controller Support and the Finished Classroom
**Builds on:** [v0.0.10](v0.0.10.md) (persistent multiplayer and private alpha)
**Followed by:** [v0.0.12](v0.0.12.md) (HUD and UI revamp)

Part of the [roadmap to v0.1.0](README.md).

---

## Overview

Version 0.0.8 established the permanent visual direction: a playable three-dimensional sketchbook
with pencil outlines (`src/render/outline/`), paper shading and light (`src/render/shading/`,
`src/render/lighting.ts`), a shared material library (`src/render/materials/`,
`src/render/style.ts`) and a licensed-asset pipeline (`src/assets/`). Only phase 1 of its six
phases was completed: Home Desk is themed, while the other territories, enemy installations and
the other home buildings keep their v0.0.7 models, and the book stack and globe are placeholders
for licensed models.

Version 0.0.9 made the game multiplayer, and v0.0.10 made that world persistent and reachable by
the developer and one invited friend.

Version 0.0.11 is the second half of the original 0.1.0 plan. It finishes v0.0.8's remaining
phases (world expansion, territory identity, home dashboard and onboarding, gameplay polish,
testing), integrates the developer-supplied Synty models and the custom aircraft and building
models, and makes the game fully playable with a standard controller.

The release has four central objectives:

1. Complete the integration of the planned Synty and custom low-poly 3D models.
2. Finish the visual composition of all nine territories.
3. Introduce comprehensive controller support through a unified input-action system.
4. Run a second two-player playtest with one player on a controller and one on keyboard and mouse.

The release must preserve the existing PlayCanvas, TypeScript and Colyseus architecture and the
v0.0.8 art direction ([ART_DIRECTION.md](../ART_DIRECTION.md)). Do not migrate to another engine,
do not introduce a new visual direction, and do not undertake the HUD, audio and menu redesigns
planned for v0.0.12 to v0.0.14.

The overriding priority is a complete, consistent and readable world rather than more content.

## 1. Synty Asset Integration

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Replace provisional environment geometry with legally acquired Synty models adapted to
the sketchbook look.

### A. Use of Synty assets

Use Synty assets primarily for:

- Classroom furniture: desks, chairs, shelves, cupboards and storage.
- Architectural components: walls, windows, doors, floor and ceiling elements.
- Background environmental objects and decorative props.
- Suitable environmental obstacles.
- Nonessential scenery and miscellaneous low-poly objects that do not need a distinctive
  paperwars.io design.

Do not use Synty models for aircraft, player buildings, defenses, landmarks or mission targets.
Those are custom (sections 2 and 3).

### B. Pipeline

- Extend the existing slots in `src/assets/manifest.ts` (today `bookStack`, `globe` and others)
  with the full environment list agreed with the developer.
- Reuse `AssetLibrary` for loading, fitting to size, matte paper shading, outlines and colliders.
- Provide reusable tools for import, conversion, positioning and optimisation (for example a
  script that validates a `.glb`, reports triangles and materials, and checks fit).
- Every slot keeps a functional placeholder so the game runs without licensed files.

### C. Licensing

- Proprietary models are never committed to the public repository. `assets/licensed/` stays
  git-ignored.
- The deployed client must still receive the licensed files. The build or deployment step copies
  them from a private location (private storage bucket, private repository or a manual upload),
  documented step by step.
- Confirm that the Synty licence permits use in a browser-delivered game build, and record the
  answer in `assets/licensed/README.md`.

### D. Honest reporting

If required packs have not been supplied, list them explicitly in the README with the slots they
fill, and keep the placeholders. Do not declare asset integration complete.

## 2. Custom Aircraft Models

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Complete the initial aircraft roster with distinctive custom models.

### A. Roster

| Aircraft | Role | Visual character | Status before v0.0.11 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dart | Starter / scout | Simple, narrow folded paper airplane | In game (`src/config.ts`) |
| Falcon | Fighter | Angular, aggressive paper fighter, F-35-inspired fold | In game |
| Swift | Multipurpose | Balanced, compact, twin guns and two bombs | In game, keeps its role |
| Widewing | Bomber | Broad wings, visibly larger payload | In game |
| Shadow | Advanced fighter | Sharp, streamlined, high-performance silhouette | New |
| Courier | Reconnaissance / support | Lightweight, stable, long-range appearance | New |

Swift is not in the GDD's example table but has been the multipurpose plane since v0.0.3. It stays
unless the developer decides otherwise.

### B. Shadow and Courier statistics

Proposed starting values, to be tuned in playtesting:

- **Shadow:** the most expensive fighter. Faster and more agile than the Falcon, stronger guns,
  lower range and health than the Swift, no bombs or a single small bomb. Must not make the
  Falcon obsolete: higher cost and a longer rebuild are its trade-offs.
- **Courier:** cheap support plane. The longest range and best stability, a light gun, no bombs,
  and a bonus on reconnaissance and delivery objectives (faster recon timers, for example).

Both join the garage, the mission fit icons, the emergency rules (the free emergency plane stays
the Dart), the mission director's suitability checks and the server's plane validation.

### C. Model requirements

Each aircraft must have:

- Distinctive geometry that reads at speed and at distance.
- Visible paper folds and creases.
- Low-poly optimisation within an agreed triangle budget.
- Consistent outline rendering (aircraft tier, boldest lines).
- Correct collision geometry.
- Weapon and payload attachment points (gun muzzles, bomb racks).
- Compatible folding and construction animations (`src/aircraft/PlaneModel.ts`,
  `PlaneShapes.ts`).
- Correct multiplayer synchronisation, including bomb racks and the fold animation on remote
  planes, which v0.0.9 lists as missing.
- Suitable damage and destruction effects (crumple and tear).

### D. Handling

Preserve existing handling and balance for Dart, Falcon, Swift and Widewing unless new geometry
requires adjustment. Aircraft must be clearly distinguishable in flight and on the selection
screen.

## 3. Custom Building and Defense Models

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Every player building and defense uses its intended custom model with all its states.

### A. Roster

| Building | Gameplay source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Aircraft hangar | Launch pad and garage | New visual structure; no new gameplay |
| Paper workshop | `workshop` in `BASE.buildings` | Production building |
| Storage box | `storage` | Capacity and protected paper |
| Ink structure | New visual (ink well or ink depot) | Visual only until ink becomes a resource in v0.0.15 |
| Radar station | `radar` | Early warning |
| Ink turret | `turret` | Light defense |
| Heavy anti-air | `launcher` at higher levels, or a heavy variant | Anti-bomber |
| Missile defense | Paper rocket launcher | Homing rockets |
| Paper store | Home Desk store from v0.0.8 | Already custom |

Enemy installations on AI territories (`src/defenses/Installation.ts`) use the same models with
hostile colours.

### B. States

Each building supports the states that apply to it:

- Construction (folding or assembling).
- Idle.
- Active operation (turret tracking, radar sweep, workshop activity).
- Damaged (darkened, bent, torn).
- Destroyed (collapsed cardboard).
- Repaired.
- Upgrades (levels 1 to 3 visibly different).

### C. Style

- Cardboard, paper folds, tape, stationery and classroom-inspired details.
- Ownership colours with symbols (blue player, red hostile, grey neutral, orange objective).
- No decorative complexity that undermines performance.
- All states must be synchronised to every player.

## 4. Territory Environment Completion

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Finish all nine territories, completing v0.0.8 phases 2 and 3.

### A. Territories

| Territory | Name | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Home Desk | Notebook (done in v0.0.8) |
| T2 | Pencil Point | To be assigned |
| T3 | Notebook Ridge | Notebook |
| T4 | Eraser Fields | To be assigned |
| T5 | Paper Citadel (AI centre) | Cardboard |
| T6 | Ink Valley | To be assigned |
| T7 | Cardboard Fort | Cardboard |
| T8 | Ruler Crossing | Graph Paper |
| T9 | Scrap Corner | Colored Paper or Art Desk |

The assignment of names and themes to territory IDs is the agent's proposal for the developer's
approval. Themes come from `src/world/territories/themes.ts` (Notebook, Cardboard, Graph Paper,
Colored Paper, Art Desk).

In multiplayer any claimable desk can be a player's home, so every territory must work both as a
home base and as a target.

### B. Requirements for every territory

- Its assigned visual theme.
- A recognisable landmark (for example a pencil cup, a giant eraser, an ink bottle, a ruler
  bridge).
- Correct environmental props and decorative geometry.
- Functional landing and construction areas, kept clear (tests already check this for Home Desk).
- Readable ownership indicators.
- Accurate collision boundaries, shared by client and server. v0.0.9 dresses homes without the
  landmark and tall props because the server does not know their collision shapes; this must be
  resolved.

### C. Classroom backdrop

- The classroom around the territories: floor, walls, windows, large furniture (Synty).
- A small number of obstacles that create interesting flight without blocking gameplay.

## 6. Performance and Asset Delivery

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** The complete world loads quickly and runs smoothly in desktop browsers.

### A. Techniques

- GLB asset loading with caching.
- Geometry and material reuse.
- Instancing for repeated props.
- Texture compression where supported (Basis or KTX2).
- Draw-call reduction (batching static scenery).
- Culling.
- Level of detail where useful.
- Loading progress feedback.
- Fallbacks when an asset fails to load.

### B. Budgets

- Agreed budgets for triangles, draw calls, outline strokes and texture memory per preset (Low,
  Medium, High in `src/render/GraphicsSettings.ts`), reported by `?perf` and
  `paperwars.assets.report()`.
- Initial load time target on a typical home connection.

### C. Delivery

All required runtime assets, licensed ones included, are available to the deployed client from
v0.0.10's environment.

## 7. Controller Support

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** paperwars.io is playable with a standard game controller without a keyboard or mouse
during normal gameplay.

### A. Gamepad detection

- Browser Gamepad API.
- Xbox-compatible and PlayStation-compatible controllers, using the standard mapping where the
  browser provides it.
- Connection and disconnection detection at any time.
- Browser-specific mapping fixes where possible, and fallback behaviour for unknown controllers.

### B. Unified input-action system

- Replace direct key checks (today `src/systems/Input.ts` and `GAME_KEYS`, and key handling in
  the UI) with named actions (`steer`, `throttleUp`, `firePrimary`, `fireSecondary`, `boost`,
  `confirm`, `back`, `emote1`, ...).
- Keyboard, mouse and controller all feed the same actions.
- Gameplay features read actions, never devices.

### C. Default controller mapping

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| Left stick | Pitch and yaw, assisted flight steering |
| Right stick | Camera look |
| RT / R2 | Primary weapon |
| LT / L2 | Secondary weapon or bomb release |
| RB / R1 | Boost |
| LB / L1 | Assisted banking modifier or contextual flight action |
| A / Cross | Confirm, launch, interact |
| B / Circle | Back, cancel |
| X / Square | Contextual action (land, repair, rearm) |
| Y / Triangle | Aircraft or mission information |
| D-pad | Menu navigation, emotes in flight |
| View / Share | Scoreboard |
| Menu / Options | Pause menu |

The mapping may change after playtesting. Rebinding belongs to v0.0.14.

### D. Analog flight handling

- Dead zones (radial and per-axis).
- Analog sensitivity and response curves.
- Optional inversion.
- Smoothing.
- Camera sensitivity and automatic recentering.
- Controller flight must feel natural, not like mouse steering mapped onto a stick. Aim
  assistance (from v0.0.5) is tuned for controller players.

### E. Controller menu navigation

Controller-only navigation for every existing functional screen:

- Aircraft selection and construction (garage).
- Mission selection.
- Base management, building placement, upgrades and repairs.
- Multiplayer lobby, world selection and joining.
- Pause, help and feedback log.

Provide visible focus states and remove hover-only interactions.

### F. Input switching

- Seamless switching between controller and keyboard and mouse.
- Control hints follow the last device used.
- No restart when a controller is connected or disconnected. Disconnecting mid-flight pauses
  input safely (in single-player, pauses the game).

### G. Fairness

Controller and mouse players must have comparable control responsiveness in combat.

## 8. Testing and Verification

**Priority:** Critical

### A. Automated tests

- All existing tests and the v0.0.10 persistence tests pass.
- New tests for the input-action layer (mapping, dead zones, curves, device switching).
- Asset tests: every manifest slot loads, placeholders work without licensed files, colliders
  match between client and server, nothing sits on runways or slots for every territory.
- Plane tests for Shadow and Courier (flight model, weapons, costs, mission fit).

### B. Browser tests

- Headless Chromium checks for loading, every territory and every building state.
- Screenshots of each territory and aircraft for developer review.

### C. Real hardware

Controller behaviour must be validated on real hardware, not only simulated:

- Flight, weapons, landing.
- Building construction and mission selection.
- Camera movement and menu navigation.
- Controller disconnection and reconnection.

### D. Second two-player playtest

- One player on a controller, one on keyboard and mouse, on the deployed server.
- The v0.0.10 acceptance sequence repeated with the new assets.
- Feedback recorded in the feedback log.

### E. Measurements

- Frame rate and frame-time stability per graphics preset on both test computers.
- Draw calls, triangles, strokes, texture memory.
- Asset loading time and download size.
- Memory consumption over a long session.

## Final Design Principle

Version 0.0.11 should make the classroom look and feel finished. Every plane should be
recognisable at a glance, every desk should be a place with its own character, and a player
holding a controller should feel as at home as one with a mouse. The world that v0.0.10 taught to
remember is now worth returning to.
