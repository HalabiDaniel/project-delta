# paperwars.io: Version 0.0.13 Development Plan

**Development stage:** Private two-player alpha (server)
**Release type:** Multiplayer structure and stability milestone
**Release theme:** Sessions: Timed, Concurrent Sessions, Random Desks, the Territory Lifecycle and a Server That Survives Restarts
**Builds on:** [v0.0.12](v0.0.12.md) (persistent, portable bases)
**Followed by:** [v0.0.14](v0.0.14.md) (going live: the server on the internet)

Part of the [roadmap to v0.1.0](README.md).

---

## Overview

v0.0.12 made the base the player's own and portable. This version builds what it is placed into:
**sessions**. The developer's model (2026-11-02):

- There is no single permanent world. There are **sessions**: each has a **time limit** and a
  **capacity** (16 players now, one per desk; up to 32 once larger classrooms arrive in v0.0.20).
- Several sessions run **at the same time**. Players come and go during a session; when one fills
  up, another opens.
- Joining gives the player a **random free desk** and places their base on it.
- When a player leaves, their desk goes through a **lifecycle**: empty for a gap, then open to
  other players, then taken by the AI if nobody comes. A joining player can replace an AI desk at
  once.
- No practice targets in sessions: everything in the room is a real plane, building or player
  (human or AI).

It also makes the server survive restarts and crashes and protects players across disconnects:
the remaining work of v0.0.10's phases 6 and 7.

The release has seven central objectives:

1. Timed sessions with a capacity, several at once, created and closed automatically.
2. Random desk placement, with the reconnect grace keeping a player on their desk.
3. The territory lifecycle: draining, empty, open, AI-held, and instant replacement of the AI.
4. Fair disconnects: a raid in progress finishes normally even if the defender leaves; no new raids
   on players who are away; a short grace after arriving.
5. Flight that continues sensibly across disconnects.
6. Saving, graceful shutdown, restoring sessions after a restart, and crash recovery.
7. A hardened server: rate limits, server-only room settings, validated messages, clear errors and
   structured logs.

## 1. Sessions

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Many short-lived shared games instead of one forever-world.

### A. What a session is

- One Colyseus room (`server/WorldRoom.ts`) running one `ServerWorld`, with an id, a start time, a
  length and a capacity.
- Provisional values (all in `src/config.ts`, `SESSION`):

| Setting | Provisional value |
|---|---|
| Length | 60 minutes |
| Capacity | 16 players (one per desk); 32 after v0.0.20 |
| Warnings before the end | 10 minutes, 2 minutes, 30 seconds |
| Joining closes | 5 minutes before the end |
| A new session opens when | every open session is full or closing |
| An empty session closes after | 2 minutes with no players |

- What lives only for the session: the session scoreboard and score, kill streaks, session
  bounties, active raids and missions in flight, AI traffic. What persists is the player's (v0.0.12):
  base, paper, planes, board, statistics, fairness timers.

### B. Ending a session

- At the end: missions in flight are settled (completed objectives paid, the rest cancelled with
  the stake refunded), raids in progress are settled as they stand, planes in the air return to the
  hangar in their current condition, bases are packed.
- Everyone sees a **session results** card (placings, kills, raids, missions, paper earned) and a
  "Join next session" button that places them in a new session on a new random desk.

### C. Choosing a session

- **Quick join** picks the open session with the most players that has space and time left; if
  there is none, it creates one.
- The lobby lists open sessions (players, time left, ping) for players who want to pick. A full
  session explains it is full.
- A player is in at most one session at a time (the v0.0.10 takeover rule already enforces one
  connection per player).
- Sessions are recorded in the database (`sessions`: id, started, ends, capacity, status) for
  restore and statistics.

## 2. Placement

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** A random free desk every time, but never a move because of a connection blip.

- Joining places the player on a **random desk among those open to players**: empty desks whose
  gap is over, and AI-held desks (section 3). The claim map is replaced by a short "You landed on…"
  card showing the desk on the mini-map.
- **Within the reconnect grace (60 s, `NET.reconnectGrace`)** a player gets the same desk, base and
  plane back.
- After that, a later join gives a new random desk. Leaving and rejoining to reroll a desk is
  allowed (it costs time, and the choice is random).
- Every desk in a session is always either a player's, the AI's, or in its empty gap.

## 3. The Territory Lifecycle

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Desks are reused fairly and quickly, and the room never feels empty.

| State | What it means | Next |
|---|---|---|
| **AI-held** | The AI's installations and pilots defend the desk with a goofy name (v0.0.12). Every desk starts the session like this unless a player is placed on it | A joining player can be placed here **at once**, replacing the AI |
| **Player-held** | A player's base is on it | The player leaves → **Draining** |
| **Draining** | The player has left or dropped. Their base stays in place, defenses active, through the reconnect grace and until any raid on it has finished and been settled | Player back within the grace → Player-held. Otherwise, once settled → base packed → **Empty** |
| **Empty** | Bare desk, nothing on it, no player may be placed here | After the **gap** → **Open** |
| **Open** | Bare desk; a joining player may be placed here | A player is placed → Player-held. Nobody after the **AI delay** → AI-held |

Provisional values (`TERRITORY_LIFECYCLE` in `src/config.ts`):

| Timer | Provisional value |
|---|---|
| Gap (Empty → Open) | 2 minutes |
| AI delay (Open → AI-held) | 5 minutes |

- Desk states are shown to everyone on the mini-map and scoreboard.
- Missions never target an Empty or Open desk; world events avoid them.
- The AI moving in or out never happens under a player's plane: installations appear and disappear
  only where no player can see them (the existing `seen` rule in `ServerWorld`).

## 4. Fair Disconnects

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Disconnecting never escapes a raid, and nobody is attacked while away.

### A. A raid in progress finishes normally

- If the defender leaves or drops during a raid, **the raid continues**: their base stays in place
  (Draining), their turrets, launchers and radar keep defending, and the raid ends as it would have
  (success, repelled or failed) with normal losses and rewards.
- The attacker's raid is never cancelled because the defender left.
- Only after the raid is settled (damage, rewards and losses written) does the base pack up and
  the desk's gap start.
- Settlement happens once, with the raid id as the idempotency key.

### B. No new raids on players who are away

- A Draining base is not offered as a new raid target, and nobody can start a raid on it.
- A player not in a session has no base in the world, so they can't be raided at all.

### C. Grace after arriving

- After being placed, a player can't be the target of a new raid for **2 minutes** (provisional,
  `PVP.arrivalGrace`), so they can get their bearings.
- The existing rules stay: new-player protection, the raid shield, pair cooldowns, decaying repeat
  rewards and kill-farming windows (`PVP`), now persisted with the player (v0.0.12).

## 5. Flight Across Disconnects

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Clear, fair rules for a plane in the air when its pilot's connection goes.

- There is no fuel: a player can fly as long as they like, land at home at any time, rearm (or not)
  and take off again to carry on with whatever they were doing.
- Rules when the connection drops (approved by the developer):

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| Back within the reconnect grace | The flight continues where the server kept it (as in v0.0.9) |
| Gone longer, not in combat | The plane glides home and is returned undamaged; an active mission fails without further penalty |
| Gone longer, in combat or on a raid | The plane is lost as if shot down; nobody earns a kill reward for it |
| Server restart while airborne | The plane returns to the hangar in its last saved condition; active missions are cancelled and their stake refunded |

- "In combat" means the plane took or dealt damage from or to another player, or was on a raid,
  within the last 10 seconds (provisional).
- A plane is never duplicated or left in an invalid place.

## 6. No Practice Targets in Sessions

**Priority:** High

- Practice targets, bullseyes and the target range are removed from multiplayer sessions. Everything
  in the room is a real plane, building or player (human or AI).
- Missions that used practice targets are replaced by missions against AI desks, AI aircraft and
  world events.
- Single-player keeps them for now; a tutorial mode may use them later (v0.0.24).

---

## 7. Saving, Shutdown, Restore and Recovery

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** A server restart or crash loses nothing that was confirmed.

- **When things are saved:** paper changes, purchases, builds, repairs, raid results and mission
  outcomes at once, in transactions (v0.0.12). Building health between hits and other frequent
  changes every 5 seconds, and at packing and shutdown.
- **Graceful shutdown** (`SIGTERM`, `SIGINT`): stop placing players, tell everyone ("Server
  restarting, back in a moment"), settle raids and missions as at a session end, save every base,
  then exit.
- **Restore after a restart:** sessions that were running are reopened for 5 minutes (provisional)
  so reconnecting players land back on their desk; players who don't come back in that time are
  placed normally next time.
- **Crash recovery:** anything not committed is discarded, so every base is in its last committed
  state; the host restarts the process automatically (v0.0.14).
- **Incompatible saves** are refused with a clear message (the save-format check from v0.0.10).

## 8. Hardening

**Priority:** Critical

- **Server-only room settings:** a client can no longer set a session's seed, protection time or
  other options when it creates or joins one; sessions are created by the server.
- **Rate limits** per player and per address: joins, commands, emotes, transfer codes and (in
  v0.0.14) feedback.
- **Validation** of every message's shape and range; malformed messages dropped and logged.
- **Clear errors** for: server unreachable, every session full, failed or withdrawn pass, an
  incompatible version, a save that can't be restored, a session ending.
- **Structured logs:** one line per event with time, session id, player id and context (joins,
  leaves, placements, raids, settlements, errors), ready for the host's log viewer in v0.0.14.
- Guests and (later, v0.0.22) signed-in players use the same join path.

## 9. Testing and Verification

**Priority:** Critical

- All existing tests pass.
- New tests:
  - several sessions at once; quick join filling one before opening another; closing empty sessions;
  - session end: missions settled, raids settled, planes home, bases packed, results shown;
  - random placement, and the same desk within the reconnect grace;
  - every lifecycle transition and timer, including a player replacing an AI desk at once;
  - a raid that continues after the defender leaves, with defenses firing and normal losses,
    settled once, and the gap starting only afterwards;
  - no new raids on Draining bases; the arrival grace;
  - the four flight-across-disconnect rules;
  - no practice targets in sessions;
  - graceful shutdown and restore; a killed process (not a graceful stop) restarting with every base
    in its last committed state;
  - rate limits, rejected room options and malformed messages.
- Bots join and leave sessions at random, survive restarts, and never duplicate a base or a reward.
- The load test (`npm run loadtest`) runs 16 players in a session with saving on, and two sessions
  at once.

## 13. Definition of Done

- [ ] Several timed sessions run at once, open and close automatically, and end with results.
- [ ] Players are placed on a random free desk and keep it through the reconnect grace.
- [ ] Desks follow the lifecycle (Draining, Empty, Open, AI-held), and a player can replace an AI desk at once.
- [ ] A raid in progress finishes normally if the defender leaves, with their defenses active, settled once.
- [ ] Players who are away can't be raided; arriving players have a grace period.
- [ ] Planes in the air follow the flight-across-disconnect rules.
- [ ] No practice targets in sessions.
- [ ] Graceful shutdown, restore after a restart and crash recovery lose nothing confirmed.
- [ ] Room settings are server-only; rate limits and validation are in place; errors are clear.
- [ ] Structured logs record every join, placement, raid, settlement and error.
- [ ] All existing and new tests pass, including the load test with saving on.

---

## Final Design Principle

A session is a fresh hour in a busy classroom; the base is what a player brings to it. Coming and
going must always be fair: nobody escapes a fight by pulling the plug, and nobody is attacked while
they are away.
