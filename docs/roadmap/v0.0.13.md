# paperwars.io: Version 0.0.13 Development Plan

**Development stage:** Private Playable Alpha
**Release type:** Audio overhaul
**Release theme:** Audio and Music Revamp: a cohesive paper-themed soundscape, positional audio and adaptive music
**Builds on:** [v0.0.12](v0.0.12.md) (HUD and UI revamp)
**Followed by:** [v0.0.14](v0.0.14.md) (menus, settings and branding)

Part of the [roadmap to v0.1.0](README.md).

---

## Overview

The game's sound today is small and synthesised. `src/ui/Sounds.ts` generates a dozen effects with the Web Audio API and no audio files: `spotted`, `lock`, `launch`, `hit`, `collapse`, `pop`, `bandit`, `raid`, `crumple`, `kill`, `whoosh` and `ink`, plus a procedural combat track (a bass line and off-beat ticks at 132 BPM) that fades in during fights. There is one output gain, a mute flag and an `unlock` call made from a user gesture to satisfy browser autoplay rules. Sounds are not positioned in 3D, there is no ambience, no engine sound per plane, no mixing between categories and no music outside combat.

By v0.0.12, the world (v0.0.11) and the interface (v0.0.12) are finished. Version 0.0.13 makes the game sound as finished as it looks.

The release has five central objectives:

1. Build an audio system with buses, mixing, ducking, voice limits and priorities.
2. Position gameplay sounds in 3D, including engines, weapons, defenses and explosions, with distance and Doppler.
3. Add adaptive music that follows the state of play.
4. Replace placeholder sounds with a cohesive, paper-themed effects library and territory ambience.
5. Deliver audio efficiently and robustly in the browser, with settings and correct licensing.

Preserve:

- The PlayCanvas, TypeScript and browser-based architecture. Use the Web Audio API directly or through PlayCanvas's sound system; do not add a heavyweight audio middleware without a documented reason.
- The authoritative Node and Colyseus server. Audio is client-side presentation driven by game events and synchronised state; the server sends no audio.
- Every existing gameplay cue: anything the player can hear today must still be heard, and the v0.0.12 subtitles must cover the new cues.
- Every existing feature and its tests.

Do not expand scope. Settings screens beyond audio volumes are v0.0.14. New gameplay is not part of this version.

## 1. Audio System

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Replace the single-output `Sounds` class with a structured audio system that every sound goes through.

### A. Buses

| Bus | Contents |
|---|---|
| Master | Everything |
| Music | Adaptive music layers, menu music |
| Effects | Weapons, impacts, explosions, engines, defenses |
| UI | Clicks, focus moves, confirmations, alerts |
| Ambience | Territory and classroom beds |
| Voice / cues | Important warnings: lock, raid, bandit, low health |

- Each bus has its own volume, mute and optional effects (filter, compression).
- The master bus has a limiter to prevent clipping during heavy combat.

### B. Mixing and ducking

- Important cues (rocket lock, raid on home, low health) duck music and ambience briefly.
- Explosions near the player duck other effects briefly.
- Menus and pause muffle gameplay audio with a low-pass filter.
- Base management mode uses a calmer mix.

### C. Voice management

- A global voice limit and per-sound limits (for example, at most N simultaneous ink bursts).
- Priorities: player's own weapons and warnings first, then nearby events, then distant events.
- Distant or low-priority voices are culled rather than cutting off important ones.

### D. Event-driven API

- Gameplay code emits named audio events (for example `weapon.ink.fire`, `defense.turret.fire`, `aircraft.crumple`), not low-level play calls.
- A data table maps events to sound assets, variations, bus, volume, pitch range, priority and spatial settings.
- The existing `SoundName` calls are migrated to events.

## 2. Positional 3D Audio

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Players can hear where things are happening.

### A. Spatial sources

- Engines of every aircraft, including remote players and AI.
- Guns, bombs, rockets and impacts.
- Turrets, rocket launchers and radar.
- Explosions, crumples and building collapses.
- Landmarks with ambient sound where appropriate.

### B. Behaviour

- Panning and distance attenuation tuned for the classroom scale.
- Doppler on fly-bys (replacing the current `whoosh` approximation), limited so it stays pleasant.
- Occlusion or low-pass for sounds behind large objects, if affordable.
- The listener follows the chase camera, with a setting to follow the plane instead.

### C. Engines

- Each of the six planes (Dart, Falcon, Swift, Widewing, Shadow, Courier) has a distinct paper-flutter engine character.
- Pitch and volume follow speed and boost.
- Damaged planes sound damaged (rattling, tearing paper).

### D. Multiplayer

- Remote aircraft use interpolated positions from `src/net/Interpolation.ts` for audio, so sound and visuals agree.
- Sounds for events decided by the server (hits, kills, destruction) play on the confirmed event, not on prediction, unless the prediction is the player's own fire.

## 3. Adaptive Music

**Priority:** Very high
**Goal:** Music that follows the game, replacing the procedural combat track.

### A. Music states

| State | Trigger |
|---|---|
| Menu / lobby | Before entering the world |
| Desk (calm) | At the home desk, choosing planes and missions |
| Base building | Base management mode |
| Free flight | Flying with no threat |
| Threat | Spotted by defenses, enemy nearby |
| Dogfight | Engaged with an enemy aircraft |
| Raid on home | Your base is under attack |
| Victory | Mission success, raid repelled |
| Defeat | Plane destroyed, mission failed |

### B. Behaviour

- Layered stems or horizontal segments with transitions on musical boundaries.
- Hysteresis so the music does not flicker between states.
- Stingers for victory, defeat, kill, medal.
- Music can be turned off independently of effects.

### C. Style

- Playful, light, classroom-inspired instrumentation (for example pizzicato, woodwinds, percussion on desks, whistling) consistent with the goofy, approachable pillar.
- Tension in combat without military bombast.

## 4. Paper-Themed Effects Library

**Priority:** Very high
**Goal:** Every sound belongs to the miniature paper world.

### A. Categories

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Aircraft | Folding (construction animation), launch, paper flutter, boost, landing, scraping |
| Weapons | Ink gun bursts, ink splats, rubber-ball bomb drops and bounces, paper rocket launches |
| Impacts | Ink hits on paper, cardboard thuds, rubber bounces |
| Destruction | Crumpling, tearing, cardboard collapse |
| Defenses | Turret fire, reload, rocket rails, radar sweep, lock tones |
| Buildings | Construction, upgrade, repair (tape, scissors, stapler), damage |
| UI | Pencil scratches, paper slides, stamps, page turns, focus ticks |
| Economy | Paper earned, spent, lost |
| Social | Emotes, playful sound effects |

### B. Requirements

- Several variations per frequent sound, with pitch and volume randomisation, to avoid repetition.
- Loudness normalised across the library.
- Short UI sounds stay subtle so they never tire.
- The existing synthesised sounds remain as fallbacks if a file fails to load.

---

## 5. Ambience

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Each territory sounds like its place in the classroom.

### A. Requirements

- A classroom bed (distant room tone, clock ticking, windows) under everything.
- Per-territory ambience matching the nine themed territories from v0.0.8 and v0.0.11 (Notebook, Cardboard, Graph Paper, Colored Paper, Art Desk and the named desks).
- Crossfades as the player flies between territories.
- Home base ambience that changes with building activity (workshop sounds when production is active).

---

## 6. Multiplayer Cues

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Players hear what matters about other players.

- Raid incoming on your base, and raid repelled.
- Rival spotted near your territory.
- Being locked onto by a player or defense.
- Kill and assist confirmations.
- A player joining, leaving or reconnecting (subtle).
- Emotes with playful sounds, subject to the per-player mute from v0.0.16 once it exists.
- Every cue has a matching visual indicator or subtitle through the v0.0.12 accessibility system.

---

## 7. Settings and Browser Behaviour

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Audio behaves correctly in browsers and respects the player.

### A. Settings

- Volume and mute per bus: master, music, effects, UI, ambience, cues.
- Mute when the tab is hidden (default on).
- Mono output option.
- Settings stored with the player profile from v0.0.10 and applied immediately. The full settings screen is v0.0.14; this version adds an audio panel to the existing pause menu in `src/ui/Menus.ts`.

### B. Browser behaviour

- Honour autoplay policies: create or resume the audio context on the first user gesture (as `unlock` does today), with a visible indicator if audio is blocked.
- Handle the audio context being suspended by the browser.
- Handle missing audio support gracefully: the game runs silently, as now.

---

## 8. Delivery and Performance

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Audio loads fast and costs little.

- Compressed formats with fallbacks (for example Opus or AAC in common containers).
- Short effects preloaded and decoded; music and long ambience streamed.
- Load only what the current state needs; preload combat sounds before entering the world.
- Audio assets included in the loading progress feedback from v0.0.11.
- A total audio download budget, documented and measured.
- CPU cost of audio measured in an eight-player fight; no audible stutter.

---

## 9. Licensing

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Every sound and piece of music is legally usable.

- Every asset has a recorded source and licence in a manifest.
- Commissioned music and sounds have written agreements covering game use and promotional use.
- Licensed packs are used within their terms; if redistribution in the public repository is not allowed, they follow the same git-ignored pipeline as licensed models (`assets/licensed/`), with functional fallbacks.
- Attributions required by licences are collected for the credits screen in v0.0.14.

---

## 10. Testing and Verification

**Priority:** Critical

### A. Automated

- Unit tests for the event-to-sound table, voice limiting, priorities and ducking rules.
- Tests for music state transitions and hysteresis.
- A check that every audio event has an asset and a licence entry.
- A check that every important cue has a subtitle or visual indicator.
- Existing tests continue to pass.

### B. Hands-on

- A listening pass through every game state: menu, desk, base, free flight, threat, dogfight, raid on home, victory, defeat.
- An eight-player bot fight measured for audio CPU and stutter.
- Two-player session: verify both players hear consistent events and positions.
- Headphones and speakers check for panning and Doppler comfort.
- Tab hidden, tab restored, autoplay blocked, audio unavailable.

## 12. Developer Responsibilities

- Commission or license the music and sound effects, and keep the agreements.
- Approve the musical direction from a short sample before full production.
- Supply licensed audio files that cannot be committed.
- Listen through the game on real hardware with headphones and speakers.
- Run a two-player session and record feedback.

## 13. AI Agent Implementation Strategy

**Priority:** Critical

| Phase | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Audit current audio, list every event that should make sound, define the event table |
| 2 | Audio system: buses, mixing, ducking, voice limits, event API; migrate existing sounds |
| 3 | Positional audio and engines, including remote aircraft |
| 4 | Effects library and ambience integration; developer checkpoint on the sound direction |
| 5 | Adaptive music |
| 6 | Multiplayer cues, settings, browser behaviour, subtitles for new cues |
| 7 | Delivery, compression, streaming, licence manifest |
| 8 | Testing, eight-player measurement, two-player playtest, fixes; developer checkpoint |

Rules:

- Work in reviewable increments.
- Do not mark a phase complete because the code compiles; verify by listening in the browser and with tests.
- Preserve every earlier feature and every existing audible cue.
- Stop at the developer checkpoints after phases 4 and 8.

## 14. Definition of Done

- [ ] All sound goes through the bus system with mixing, ducking, voice limits and priorities.
- [ ] Gameplay code emits audio events; no direct low-level play calls remain.
- [ ] Engines, weapons, defenses and explosions are positioned in 3D, with Doppler on fly-bys.
- [ ] Each plane has a distinct engine sound that follows speed, boost and damage.
- [ ] Adaptive music covers every listed state, with clean transitions.
- [ ] No placeholder sounds remain; synthesised sounds exist only as fallbacks.
- [ ] Every territory has its ambience.
- [ ] Multiplayer cues play for raids, rivals, locks, kills and connections.
- [ ] Every important cue has a visual indicator or subtitle.
- [ ] Per-bus volume, mute, tab-hidden mute and mono settings work and persist.
- [ ] Autoplay restrictions and missing audio support are handled gracefully.
- [ ] Audio download size is within budget and music streams.
- [ ] No audible stutter in an eight-player fight.
- [ ] Every asset has a licence entry.
- [ ] All existing tests and the new audio tests pass.
- [ ] The developer has approved the sound direction and completed a two-player session.

---

## Final Design Principle

paperwars.io should sound like paper coming to life: flutter, crumple, splat and bounce, in a classroom that hums around you, with music that leans in when a fight starts and relaxes when it ends. Every sound should tell the player something, and none of it should tire them.
