# paperwars.io: Version 0.0.16 Development Plan

**Development stage:** Private Playable Alpha (pre-release)
**Release type:** Major multiplayer gameplay milestone
**Release theme:** AI Escorts, Bounties, Cooperation, Deeper Raids, Rivalries and Playful Social Interaction
**Builds on:** [v0.0.15](v0.0.15.md) (economy and content depth)
**Followed by:** [v0.0.17](v0.0.17.md) (world scale and environments)

Part of the [roadmap to v0.1.0](README.md).

---

## Overview

Version 0.0.9 introduced player-versus-player combat, raids on player bases with capped losses,
rival mission cards (raid, scout, shoot down, intercept), kill and assist credit, emotes, a
scoreboard and a kill feed. Version 0.0.10 made that world persistent, and v0.0.15 gave it a
three-resource economy worth fighting over.

What is still missing is the part of the GDD that turns isolated fights into stories: the
mission director that notices who is threatening whom and offers fair bounties (GDD §11.5,
§11.6), temporary cooperation between players without formal teams (§11.7), purchasable AI escorts
(§10), rivalries that shape future missions (§12.3) and the goofy, non-destructive interactions
that keep conflict playful (§7.2 mischief, §12.4).

Version 0.0.16 has six central objectives:

1. Add **purchasable, single-use AI escort aircraft**, capped and weaker than human pilots.
2. Upgrade the **mission director** to track rivalries, aggression and relative strength, and to
   issue **fair bounties** with shared rewards.
3. Offer **cooperation**: joint attacks, escorting another player's bomber and joint defense.
4. Make **raids deeper**: multi-structure targets, escorted bombers and raids in stages.
5. Record **reputation and rivalries** and use them in mission generation.
6. Add **playful social features** (rival banners, ink markings, mischief missions, more emotes)
   together with **mute and report** tools and **anti-farming** checks.

The release must preserve:

- The PlayCanvas, TypeScript and browser-based client. Do not migrate engines.
- The authoritative Node.js and Colyseus server; every new system is decided by the server
  (GDD §16.2 lists AI escort behaviour explicitly).
- The PostgreSQL persistence from v0.0.10 and the economy from v0.0.15.
- The fairness rules from v0.0.9: new-player protection, raid shields, pair cooldowns, decaying
  rewards and offline protection (`PVP` in `src/config.ts`, `server/world/Attacks.ts`).

Do not add formal alliances or friends lists (v0.0.18), accounts or public moderation queues
(v0.0.19), or text chat in any version before v0.1.0.

## 1. AI Escort Aircraft

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Let players buy a small number of weaker AI wingmen for one sortie, supporting human
piloting without replacing it (GDD §10).

### A. Escort roster

| Escort | Based on | Role | Cost (provisional) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper Wingman | Dart AI pilot | Follows, distracts defenses, light guns | Low paper, low ink |
| Guard Falcon | Falcon AI pilot | Attacks nearby hostile aircraft, protects bombers | Medium paper, medium ink |
| Spotter | Courier AI pilot | Limited reconnaissance, marks threats on the mini-map | Low paper |

- Escorts use the AI pilots from v0.0.5 (`src/ai/AiAircraft.ts`, `src/ai/Gunnery.ts`,
  `src/ai/FlightPlans.ts`) and the same flight model, health and weapons as other aircraft.
- Escorts fly at reduced skill (rookie or regular, never veteran), with lower durability and
  weapon effectiveness than a human-flown plane of the same type (GDD §10.3).

### B. Economic rules

- Escorts are bought at launch with resources and are single-use: they are removed at the end of
  the sortie, even if they survived (GDD §10.2).
- Maximum two escorts per sortie (GDD §10.3, §10.4); the cap is in `src/config.ts`.
- Escorts are not refunded when destroyed and do not award kill rewards to the opponent beyond a
  small fixed amount, so they cannot be farmed.

### C. Behaviour

- Orders: follow, attack my target, protect the bomber, cover my retreat. Orders are sent as
  commands; the server runs the escort.
- Escorts cannot complete mission objectives on their own: bombs dropped or recon done by an
  escort do not count (GDD §10.3).
- Escorts leave with their owner: if the owner is shot down, disconnects or recalls, the escorts
  peel off and are removed.
- Escorts never enter an offline player's territory and never start raids.

### D. Presentation

- Escort markers with a distinct icon in the v0.0.12 HUD, a small order wheel usable from
  controller and keyboard, and voice-free audio cues from the v0.0.13 system.

### E. Synchronization

- Escort state is part of the server world and is interpolated on clients like other AI aircraft.
- Escort count per world is bounded so bandwidth stays predictable (GDD §16.4).

## 2. Mission Director Upgrade

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Turn the v0.0.6 director (`src/director/MissionDirector.ts`) and the v0.0.9 rival cards
(`server/world/PvpMissions.ts`) into a multiplayer director that creates fair opportunities for
conflict (GDD §11).

### A. Tracked player situation (GDD §11.2)

- Current resources (all three), aircraft owned, recent success and failure, preferred mission
  categories, aggression (raids launched, kills on weaker players), territory condition, relative
  strength, online status and previous interactions.
- The situation is computed and stored on the server; it survives restarts.

### B. Tiers by player strength

- Newcomers: low-risk missions, protection from disproportionate attacks (GDD §11.3).
- Intermediate players: harder targets, bonuses for taking on stronger opponents (GDD §11.4).
- Advanced players: demanding missions; eligible for bounties because of aggressive actions or
  demonstrated threat, never because of wealth alone (GDD §11.5).

### C. Bounties (GDD §11.6)

- A bounty is always tied to a specific, fair objective:

| Bounty objective | Triggered by | Reward shared among |
|---|---|---|
| Destroy a hostile bomber | A high-threat player's active raid | Players who damaged the bomber |
| Prevent a raid | Repeated raids by one player | The defender and helpers |
| Damage an exposed installation | A dominant player's forward defenses | Contributors |
| Intercept a high-threat player | Kill streaks on weaker players | Contributors |

- Rewards are split by contribution using the damage log (`creditFor` in
  `server/world/Attacks.ts`).
- A player cannot place, claim or profit from a bounty on themselves or on a cooperating account;
  the anti-farming rules in section 7 apply.
- Bounties expire and are never placed on offline players.

### D. Generation method

- Explicit rules, templates, eligibility checks and weighted selection (GDD §11.8).
- The director never predetermines a battle outcome (GDD §11.1).
- Director decisions are logged for the developer's debug panel.

## 3. Cooperation Offers

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Encourage temporary cooperation between players without formal teams (GDD §11.7, §12.2).

### A. Offer types

- **Joint attack:** two intermediate players receive the same attack offer against a powerful
  player's bomber or exposed installation; the target receives a defense mission.
- **Escort another player's bomber:** a player who launches a raid or a bombing mission can open
  it to escorts; another player accepts an escort card and is paid if the bomber survives and
  completes its objective. (This was listed as missing in the v0.0.9 known limitations.)
- **Joint defense:** when a base is raided, nearby online players can receive a defense card to
  help intercept; the defender keeps priority on the defense reward.

### B. Rules

- Cooperation is opt-in for both sides; a player can decline escorts on their mission.
- Shared rewards are split by contribution, paid once, decided by the server.
- Cooperation cards respect all existing protections: no joint attacks on new or offline players.
- The board keeps its card limit (`PVP.cards`), so cooperation does not crowd out a player's own
  missions.

## 4. Deeper Raids

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Make attacks on built bases more interesting and readable (GDD §8, the original v0.0.10
roadmap entry).

### A. Multi-structure targets

- A raid can require hits on several buildings (for example storage and radar) rather than one
  target, extending `RaidTarget` in `server/world/PvpMissions.ts`.
- Targets are chosen from what is actually standing on the victim's base.

### B. Escorted raids

- Raiders may bring AI escorts (section 1) and accept human escorts (section 3).
- The defender is warned about the escort count when the raid is detected.

### C. Staged raids

- Optional two-stage raids: suppress a radar or turret first, then strike the store.
- Each stage has its own clock; failure in stage one ends the raid with a reduced partial reward
  (never profitable for deliberate failure, GDD §7.5).

### D. Fairness

- Loss caps per raid (now per resource, from v0.0.15), shields, pair cooldowns and repeat factors
  apply to the whole raid, not per stage.
- Damaged buildings persist and are repaired with resources as in v0.0.7.
- The defense report explains what was hit in each stage and what decided the raid.

## 5. Reputation and Rivalries

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Record notable interactions so the world remembers who fought whom (GDD §12.3).

### A. What is recorded

- Successful raids and defenses, interceptions, kills and assists between specific players,
  repeated encounters and cooperation.
- Stored per player pair on the server and persisted.

### B. How it is used

- Rival call-outs: a "nemesis" label for the player who has beaten you most recently or most often.
- The director weights retaliation and rematch missions towards rivals, within the protection
  rules.
- A reputation summary per player (defender, raider, ace, helper) shown in the scoreboard and the
  profile panel.

### C. Limits

- Reputation never changes combat stats.
- Rivalry-based offers never override new-player protection, shields, cooldowns or offline
  protection.
- Players can see why a mission was offered ("rematch with ...").

## 6. Playful Social Interaction

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Add the goofy, non-destructive interactions of GDD §7.2 and §12.4, with safety tools.

### A. Expressive features

- More emotes on top of the v0.0.9 set (`EMOTES` in `src/net/protocol.ts`), rate-limited as today.
- Rival banners: a player's banner flies over their home desk and can be targeted by mischief.
- Temporary ink markings: ink splats on aircraft and banners that fade after a short time.

### B. Mischief missions (GDD §7.2)

- Mark an opponent's banner with ink, fly through their territory unharmed, complete a teasing
  stunt near their base.
- Mischief never destroys resources or buildings.
- Mischief missions are rate-limited per target and never target offline or newly joined players,
  so they cannot become targeted harassment.

### C. Mute and report

- Mute another player's emotes and markings (locally and persistently per identity).
- Report a player with a reason category; reports are stored on the server for the developer.
  (The full moderation queue and admin tools come in v0.0.19.)
- No free text chat.

## 7. Anti-Farming

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Prevent cooperating accounts from generating unlimited resources (GDD §8.7, §13.6).

### A. Detection

- Track repeated reward flows between the same pair of players (kills, raids, bounties, escorts,
  cooperation payouts).
- Flag patterns such as one player repeatedly losing expensive planes to the same opponent, raids
  without defense, or bounties claimed by the same pair.

### B. Response

- Apply decaying rewards (as `PVP.killRepeat` and `PVP.repeat` do today) to every new reward source.
- Pause rewards between a flagged pair and log it for the developer; do not auto-ban.

### C. Verification

- Extend the v0.0.15 campaign simulator with an exploiter pair profile and assert that farming
  earns less than normal play.

## 8. Testing and Verification

**Priority:** Critical

### A. Automated tests

- Escort purchase, cap, single-use removal, no objective credit, removal on owner loss.
- Bounty placement, eligibility, expiry, shared payout, no self-bounty.
- Cooperation offers, opt-in, shared rewards.
- Multi-structure and staged raids, caps across stages.
- Rivalry recording and persistence across restarts.
- Mischief rules and rate limits; mute and report storage.
- Anti-farming detection with simulated exploiter pairs.
- All earlier tests keep passing.

### B. Bots and load

- Extend `server/bots/` so bots buy escorts, accept cooperation cards and launch staged raids.
- Run an eight-seat session (two humans and six bots) and the existing load test with escorts
  active; record bandwidth and server tick time in the README.

### C. Two-player playtest

- The developer and the invited friend play sessions with bots filling the world and report
  whether bounties and cooperation create interesting moments and whether anything feels unfair.

## 11. AI Agent Implementation Strategy

**Priority:** Critical

| Phase | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Audit v0.0.15 multiplayer systems; regression baseline; design notes for the director upgrade |
| 2 | AI escorts: purchase, behaviour, synchronization, removal |
| 3 | Director upgrade: player situation, tiers, bounties |
| 4 | Cooperation offers: joint attack, escort a bomber, joint defense |
| 5 | Deeper raids: multi-structure, escorted, staged |
| 6 | Reputation and rivalries |
| 7 | Social features, mischief missions, mute and report |
| 8 | Anti-farming detection and simulator checks |
| 9 | Bots, load tests, two-player playtest, fixes |

- Work in reviewable increments with a checkpoint for the developer after phases 2, 5 and 9.
- Do not mark a phase complete because the code compiles; verify with tests, bots and real play.
- Preserve every working feature from earlier versions.
- Keep every rule number in `src/config.ts` and document it in `docs/MULTIPLAYER.md`.

## 12. Definition of Done

- [ ] AI escorts can be bought, are capped at two, are weaker than humans and are removed after the sortie.
- [ ] Escorts cannot complete objectives and are run entirely by the server.
- [ ] The director tracks player situation and offers tiered missions.
- [ ] Bounties are tied to fair objectives, shared by contribution and cannot be farmed.
- [ ] Joint attacks, bomber escorts and joint defense work and pay correctly.
- [ ] Multi-structure, escorted and staged raids work within the existing fairness rules.
- [ ] Rivalries and reputation are recorded, persisted and used in mission generation.
- [ ] Rival banners, ink markings, new emotes and mischief missions are playable and non-destructive.
- [ ] Players can mute and report others.
- [ ] Anti-farming checks pass in the simulator and in tests.
- [ ] An eight-seat session with escorts stays within the bandwidth and tick budget.
- [ ] All earlier and new automated tests pass.
- [ ] The developer and invited friend have played the new systems and their feedback is addressed.
- [ ] `docs/MULTIPLAYER.md` and the README describe the new rules.

---

## Final design principle

The mission director creates opportunities for conflict and cooperation; the players create the
stories. Every bounty, escort and rivalry must be fair, readable and impossible to farm, and
every playful interaction must stay playful.
