# paperwars.io: Version 0.0.23 Development Plan

**Development stage:** Private alpha (expanding invited group)
**Release type:** World feature
**Release theme:** World Conditions: fans that destroy planes, wind, rain that soaks paper, day and night, lights out, and things that burst
**Builds on:** [v0.0.22](v0.0.22.md) (maps)
**Followed by:** [v0.0.24](v0.0.24.md) (world scale)

Part of the [roadmap to v0.1.0](README.md). Added on 2026-11-06 (see
[the decisions](README.md#decisions-of-2026-11-06-world)).

---

## Overview

After v0.0.22 there are nine maps, but they stand still. The furniture never moves, the air is
always calm and the light never changes.

Version 0.0.23 brings the maps to life with **world conditions**: things in the world that change
how flying feels and that have nothing to do with weapons or combat. A paper plane is light and
fragile, which is the whole idea of this version: moving air pushes it around, spinning blades
destroy it, water soaks it, and darkness hides it.

| Condition | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Ceiling fans** | Indoor | The blades destroy a plane instantly. A slight, constant downdraft under them |
| **Small fans** | Indoor | The blades destroy a plane instantly. A strong wind cone that sweeps side to side, so it keeps changing direction |
| **Other moving air** | Both | Updrafts over vents, radiators, steam, burners and hot asphalt; crosswinds at open windows; outdoor gusts |
| **Rain** | Outdoor | Comes and goes. Soaks planes: heavier handling, and a soaked plane has a limited life |
| **Indoor sprinklers** | Indoor | Like rain, but rarer and shorter |
| **Fog** | Outdoor | Shorter view and detection range |
| **Day and night** | Outdoor | The sun sets and rises during a session |
| **Lights out** | Indoor | The room goes very dark; bases keep their lights; a few planes have night vision |
| **Interactive objects** | Per map | Lab beakers burst, Bunsen burners burn, garden sprinklers soak, birds get in the way |

The release has seven central objectives:

1. One **airflow system** that every source of moving air uses, visible and the same for
   everyone.
2. **Fans**, ceiling and small, as instant-destruction hazards with their own wind.
3. **Water and soak**: rain showers that come and go, rare indoor sprinklers, garden sprinklers,
   and soaked paper that handles worse and eventually falls apart.
4. **Light**: outdoor day and night, indoor lights out, base lights, and night vision on a few
   planes.
5. **Interactive objects** that react when planes hit them.
6. A **conditions director** per session that schedules, announces and limits all of it.
7. Fairness: everything is telegraphed, the same for everyone, never targeted at a player, and
   never on top of a runway or launch pad.

Preserve:

- The flight model and maneuvers (v0.0.16): conditions add forces and handling changes through
  the shared, deterministic model.
- The server's authority and the prediction model: every condition is decided by the server and
  predicted identically by the pilot's game.
- The economy, missions and world events (v0.0.20): conditions are not events that hand out
  rewards; they change the world those events happen in.
- Every existing feature and its tests.

**Not combat:** nothing in this version is a weapon. Players can still lure each other into a fan
or a downpour, which is part of the fun.

## 1. The Airflow System

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Moving air that every machine computes identically, and that players can see.

### A. Air volumes

Each source of moving air is an **air volume** in the map data (the v0.0.22 condition anchors):

| Shape | Used by |
|---|---|
| Column (down) | Ceiling fans |
| Cone (sweeping) | Small fans |
| Column (up) | AC vents, radiators, kitchen steam, Bunsen burners, the barbecue, reading lamps, sun-heated asphalt |
| Slab (sideways) | Open windows, the drinks fridge's cold draft, the fume hood's pull |
| Field (gusts) | Outdoor maps: a slow, map-wide wind with gusts |

Each has a strength, a falloff to its edges and, where it moves (a sweeping fan, gusts), a pattern
driven by the **server's session clock and seed**. The pilot's game, the server and the AI
compute the same push for the same step.

### B. In the flight model

Air adds a push to the plane's velocity each step, scaled by the plane's weight (light planes are
pushed more; provisional, from the existing flight profile). Climbing in an updraft is free height.
A downdraft makes it harder to hold altitude. Air never takes control away from the player, and
the flight model's snapshot covers the state it needs.

### C. Seeing the air

- Moving air is always visible: dust specks and tiny paper bits stream along it, thicker where it
  is strong, and a sweeping fan's cone shows where it points.
- A small **wind marker** on the HUD shows the air the plane is in right now.
- Each source also has a sound (fans hum, vents hiss, gusts whistle), using the v0.0.18 audio
  system.

## 2. Fans

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** The classic classroom hazard: beautiful to fly near, deadly to touch.

### A. Ceiling fans

- On indoor maps (classroom, lab, library, cafeteria, gym, bedroom), hanging from the ceiling.
- The **blades destroy a plane instantly** on contact, whatever its health.
- A **slight, constant downdraft** under each fan.
- They spin all session; their speed can change (off, slow, fast) on the conditions director's
  schedule, always with a visible wind-down or wind-up first.

### B. Small fans

- Desk and floor fans on indoor maps (the teacher's desk, the lab, the bedroom, the gym's wall
  fans).
- The **blades destroy a plane instantly** too. The cage around them is a normal collision.
- A **strong cone of wind** in front, much stronger than a ceiling fan's, that **sweeps side to
  side**, so the push near it keeps changing direction. Some switch speed on the director's
  schedule.

### C. Fair danger

- Fan blades are a collision volume with no damage roll: touching them is a destruction, counted
  as a crash (no kill credit, unless another player's hit sent the plane in within the last
  seconds, which credits that player under the v0.0.9 assist rules).
- No fan is placed near a runway, a launch pad, a base slot or a take-off path (v0.0.22's
  placement rules).
- Fans are always on screen as what they are: spinning, humming, with their airflow showing. The
  first time a player flies near one, a tip says "Fan blades destroy planes."
- AI planes avoid fan blades, and use the airflow around them.

## 3. Water and Soak

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Paper and water don't mix: water changes how a plane flies and how long it lasts.

### A. Soak

Every plane has a **soak** level, from dry to soaked:

- Water raises it: flying through rain, a sprinkler's spray or an indoor sprinkler shower.
- Soak makes the plane **heavier**: slower to turn and accelerate, and a lower top speed, scaling
  with soak.
- **Limited life:** a plane that reaches full soak goes soggy and falls apart. It counts as a
  crash, under the normal loss rules. A HUD **soak meter** next to the health bar shows how close
  it is, with a warning as it fills.
- Soak goes down out of the water: slowly in the open, faster under cover (under tables, roofs,
  the slide), faster still next to heat (radiators, burners, the barbecue, sunny asphalt), and
  completely when the plane lands at home and is rearmed.
- How fast each plane soaks is provisional per aircraft (bigger planes take longer to soak through).

### B. Rain (outdoor maps)

- Rain is **always temporary**: it **comes and goes** during a session as **showers**, never
  lasting the whole session. Each shower builds up, lasts a few minutes (provisional) and clears.
- Showers are light or heavy; heavy rain soaks faster and adds gusts.
- The conditions director announces a shower before it starts (section 6), so players can head
  for cover or home.
- Puddles form on the ground during rain (visual only).

### C. Indoor sprinklers

- The rain alternative for indoor maps, and **rarer**: a "fire drill" where the ceiling sprinklers
  come on for a short time, with an alarm light flashing first.
- Same soak rules as rain, but shorter and heavier.

### D. Garden sprinklers (Backyard)

- Rotating sprinklers on the lawn that sweep jets of water: flying through a jet soaks fast. They
  follow the session clock, so everyone sees the same jets.

## 4. Light: Day, Night and Lights Out

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Darkness that changes how a fight plays, without ever making the game unplayable.

### A. Day and night (outdoor maps)

- Outdoor maps have a **time of day** that moves through a session: day, dusk, night and dawn,
  compressed into the session's length. Each session starts at a different time of day.
- At night the map is dark but readable: moonlight, the skyline's lit windows, and the lights on
  bases.

### B. Lights out (indoor maps)

- An indoor event: the room lights go off for a while (provisional: a minute or two), then come back
  on. The conditions director schedules it, with a flicker as a warning.
- The room goes **very dark but stays slightly visible**: the shapes of furniture and planes can
  still be made out up close.

### C. What stays lit

- **Bases keep their lights:** the existing building lights stay on, and buildings show **red
  flashing beacon dots**, so every base is visible in the dark and players can find home.
- Turrets' muzzle flashes and ink tracers show when they fire.
- Each plane has small **navigation lights** that are visible only up close, so a plane can be
  seen when it is near but not across the map. The developer decides at the checkpoint whether
  planes get them or stay fully dark.
- Rank trails and auras (v0.0.17) dim in the dark to the same level as the plane, so cosmetics
  never make a plane easier to see.

### D. Night vision

- A few planes have **night vision**: in the dark, their pilot sees a brightened, tinted view of
  the world. Everyone else sees only what the light allows.
- Provisional: the recon and stealth planes, **Dart, Specter, Lightning and Ghost**. Their roles
  already suit seeing without being seen. It is a new aircraft trait in `src/config.ts`, shown on
  the garage card.
- Night vision changes only that pilot's screen. It does not make anything visible to anyone else.

### E. Darkness and the systems

- Defenses keep detecting by radar as before; darkness doesn't blind them.
- AI pilots see less far in the dark unless their plane has night vision.
- The brightness setting can lift the dark only up to a fixed limit, the same for everyone, so no
  one can turn the night off.

## 5. Interactive Objects and Other Conditions

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** A few objects per map that react when planes meet them.

### A. Objects

| Object | Map | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Lab beakers** | Science Lab | Crashing into one bursts it in a small explosion that damages the plane that hit it and planes very close by. Liquid splashes; the beaker refills after a while |
| **Bunsen burners** | Science Lab | A flame that damages planes flying through it, with a rising updraft of heat above |
| **Garden sprinklers** | Backyard | Section 3D |
| **Birds** | Outdoor maps | Small flocks (pigeons on the Rooftop) that sit on furniture and scatter when planes come close. A plane that flies into a bird takes a bump of damage |

- The server decides when an object bursts, burns or scatters, so everyone sees the same thing.
  Objects reset on a timer.
- Hitting an object is never credited as a kill. Planes nearby when a beaker bursts are damaged by
  the burst, and if they are shot down within a few seconds the shooter gets normal credit.

### B. Other outdoor conditions

| Condition | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Fog** | Comes and goes like rain. Shorter view distance, and defenses' detection ranges and players' markers shrink with it |
| **Gusts** | Part of the outdoor wind field: stronger in some sessions and during heavy rain |
| **Sun thermals** | On sunny days, updrafts over the playground's asphalt and the rooftop |
| **Thunderstorm** (optional) | Rare, during heavy rain: lightning strikes the tallest objects after a visible warning flash, destroying planes very close to the strike. The developer decides whether it stays |

## 6. The Conditions Director

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Conditions that come at good moments, are always announced, and never pile up.

- Each map has a **conditions profile**: which conditions it can have and how often (for
  example: Backyard has showers, fog, sprinklers and day and night; the Gym has fans, lights out
  and rare indoor sprinklers).
- The server schedules them per session from a seed: **at most one major condition at a time**
  (a shower, fog, lights out or indoor sprinklers), with quiet periods in between, and none in the
  first minutes of a session.
- **Every condition is announced** before it starts: a forecast card ("Rain in 1 min"), a short
  sound, and a sign in the world (clouds gathering, the lights flickering, the sprinkler alarm
  light). The HUD shows the current and next condition.
- Conditions apply to **everyone in the area equally**. They never target a player, and no
  condition is triggered because of what a specific player is doing.
- Raids, missions and world events go on during conditions. The mission director can prefer
  missions that suit them (a recon mission in fog).
- The admin endpoint (v0.0.14) can turn conditions off per session for testing, and the dashboard
  gets the switch in v0.0.27.

## 7. Multiplayer, AI and Performance

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Conditions that look the same on every screen, cost little and are understood by the AI.

### A. Multiplayer

- Fans, gusts, sprinklers and the time of day are computed from the session clock and seed, so
  they cost no bandwidth and every client draws the same thing.
- Scheduled conditions (showers, fog, lights out, indoor sprinklers) and object events (a beaker
  bursting) are announced by the server with the step they start on.
- Soak is part of the plane's flight state and snapshot. Prediction tests cover airflow, soak and
  a destruction by fan blades.

### B. AI

- AI pilots avoid fan blades, use updrafts, take cover or go home when soaked, and see less far in
  the dark (unless their plane has night vision).
- AI aces use conditions on purpose: they climb in updrafts and raid in fog.

### C. Performance

- Rain, fog, dust specks and darkness have per-preset budgets (fewer particles on Low), and frame
  time with the heaviest condition stays within each map's v0.0.22 budget.

### D. Accessibility

- Lightning, alarm lights and beacon dots respect the reduced-flashing setting (they glow instead
  of flashing).
- Every condition has a visual and a text cue, and a sound with a subtitle (v0.0.15, v0.0.18).

## 8. Testing and Verification

### A. Automated

- Air volumes: the push at sample points, falloff and the sweeping and gust patterns; client and
  server agree step by step.
- Fan blades destroy planes instantly at any health; the cage is a normal collision; crediting a
  shooter within the assist window.
- Soak: build-up per source, handling changes, destruction at full soak, drying under cover, near
  heat and at home, per aircraft.
- Rain showers always end; at most one major condition at a time; the quiet start; every condition
  is announced before it begins.
- Lights out: bases' lights and beacons stay on; the brightness cap; night vision only changes the
  pilot's own screen; cosmetic effects dim with the plane.
- Objects: bursts and resets are server-decided and identical on clients; no kill credit for
  hitting one.
- No hazard anywhere near a runway, launch pad, slot or take-off path, on every map.
- All earlier tests pass.

### B. Browser

- Screenshots of every condition on every map where it can happen, on High and Low.
- Bots flying a full session with conditions on every map; frame time per condition.

### C. Playtests

- The developer, the friend and the invited group play sessions with conditions on every map.
- Questions: Did a fan ever kill you unfairly? Did you see rain coming? Is lights out exciting or
  frustrating? Do updrafts and soak change how you fly?

## 9. Release Scope and Boundaries

Defer:

| Deferred item | Owning version |
|---|---|
| Conditions on the 32-player layouts | v0.0.24 |
| Conditions in the admin dashboard | v0.0.27 |
| LiveOps condition events ("storm weekend") | After v0.1.0 |
| Seasons and snow | After v0.1.0 |
| Players controlling conditions (switching fans or lights) | Not planned: conditions belong to the world |

Not in this version (decided 2026-11-06): chalk dust clouds, spilling ink bottles, toppling
stacks of paper or cups, rubber-band launchers, the school bell.

Existing systems from earlier versions must keep working.

## 10. Developer Responsibilities

- Approve the conditions per map and how often they happen.
- Approve the soak values, the rain shower length and the lights-out length.
- Decide whether planes get navigation lights in the dark, and which planes have night vision.
- Decide whether the thunderstorm stays.
- Supply or approve models and effects for fans, sprinklers, beakers, burners and birds.
- Playtest sessions with conditions and report anything that feels unfair.

## 11. AI Agent Implementation Strategy

| Phase | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Audit the flight model, maps and condition anchors; the air volume format; the regression baseline |
| 2 | The airflow system in the flight model, its visuals and the HUD wind marker; updrafts, windows and outdoor wind |
| 3 | Ceiling and small fans: blades, downdraft, sweeping cones, speed changes. **Checkpoint:** the developer flies around fans |
| 4 | Soak, rain showers, indoor and garden sprinklers, drying, the soak meter |
| 5 | Day and night, lights out, base lights and beacons, navigation lights, night vision, the brightness cap |
| 6 | Interactive objects (beakers, burners, birds), fog, gusts, sun thermals, the optional thunderstorm |
| 7 | The conditions director, announcements, multiplayer sync, AI behaviour, performance and accessibility. **Checkpoint:** the playtest with conditions on every map |

- Work in reviewable increments, each with screenshots at a developer checkpoint.
- Do not mark a phase complete because the code compiles; verify it with tests, browser runs and
  play.
- Preserve every working feature from earlier versions.
- Record decisions and open questions in the README.

## 12. Definition of Done

- [ ] Air moves planes identically on every machine, and it is always visible.
- [ ] Ceiling and small fans destroy planes instantly on their blades and push planes with their
      wind; small fans sweep.
- [ ] Rain comes and goes in showers on outdoor maps; indoor sprinklers are rare; garden
      sprinklers sweep.
- [ ] Soak changes handling, destroys a fully soaked plane, and dries as designed.
- [ ] Outdoor maps go from day to night and back; indoor maps have lights out; bases stay lit with
      beacons; night vision works on the approved planes.
- [ ] Beakers, burners and birds react as designed, decided by the server.
- [ ] The conditions director announces every condition, never stacks major ones, and never
      targets a player.
- [ ] No hazard is near a runway, launch pad, slot or take-off path on any map.
- [ ] Performance stays within each map's budget with conditions on.
- [ ] All earlier automated tests pass.
- [ ] The developer and the invited group have played sessions with conditions on every map.

---

## Final Design Principle

The world should feel alive and a little dangerous for something made of paper, and every danger
should be one the player saw coming. Losing a plane to a fan should make a player laugh and fly
more carefully, not feel cheated.
