# paperwars.io: Version 0.0.17 Development Plan

**Development stage:** Private Playable Alpha (pre-release)
**Release type:** World scale and technical scalability milestone
**Release theme:** Larger Worlds, New Environments, Interest-Based Networking and Regional Servers
**Builds on:** [v0.0.16](v0.0.16.md) (advanced multiplayer systems)
**Followed by:** [v0.0.18](v0.0.18.md) (customization and social)

Part of the [roadmap to v0.1.0](README.md).

---

## Overview

Up to v0.0.16, every multiplayer world is the same classroom: a 3 x 3 grid of territories
(`src/world/Grid.ts`, `src/world/WorldGenerator.ts`) with eight claimable desks and an AI-held
centre (`PVP.centre` in `src/config.ts`), served by one server process that sends the full world
state to every client at a fixed patch rate (`NET.patchMs`, `server/WorldRoom.ts`).

The GDD suggests 8 to 12 concurrent players and 12 to 16 territories per session (§3.1), later
environments beyond the classroom (§2: bedrooms, desks, bookshelves, pencil cases), and
interest-based network updates as the game expands (§16.4). The public release in v0.1.0 also
needs players outside one region to get a playable connection.

Version 0.0.17 has seven central objectives:

1. Support worlds of **12 to 16 territories** with matching layouts, balance and protection rules.
2. Add **three new environments** (bedroom, library, art room) with the same visual quality as the
   finished classroom from v0.0.11.
3. Introduce **interest-based network updates**, so bandwidth scales with what each player can
   see rather than with world size.
4. Prepare **regional servers** and **ping-based world selection**.
5. Keep performance acceptable on a defined **minimum specification** through level of detail,
   streaming and instancing.
6. Add a **hover and VTOL flight mode** to the shared flight model.
7. Add two aircraft built on it: the **Jumper** (AV-8B, VTOL fighter) and the **Wasp** (AH-6,
   light attack helicopter), bringing the roster to twelve.

The release must preserve:

- The PlayCanvas, TypeScript and browser-based client. Do not migrate engines.
- The authoritative Node.js and Colyseus server, the prediction and interpolation model from
  v0.0.9 (`src/net/Prediction.ts`, `src/net/Interpolation.ts`) and the persistence from v0.0.10.
- The pencil-outline renderer, paper materials and asset pipeline from v0.0.8 and v0.0.11.
- All fairness, economy and director rules from v0.0.9, v0.0.15 and v0.0.16.

Do not build matchmaking, automatic world creation or campaign resets (v0.0.19). This version
makes larger worlds and multiple regions technically possible and playable; v0.0.19 decides how
players are placed into them.

## 1. Larger Worlds: 12 to 16 Territories

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Let one world hold 12 to 16 territories and up to 12 concurrent players, while staying
readable and fair (GDD §3.1).

### A. World layouts

- Replace the fixed 3 x 3 grid with layout definitions: a set of territory positions, the
  AI-held territories, the claimable homes and the runway directions.
- Provide at least three layouts:

| Layout | Territories | Claimable homes | AI-held | Max players |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Classroom (existing) | 9 | 8 | 1 (centre) | 8 |
| Classroom Large | 12 | 10 | 2 | 10 |
| Classroom Wide | 16 | 12 | 4 | 12 |

- Layouts live in data (alongside `src/world/WorldGenerator.ts`), not in code branches.
- The world size chosen for a world is stored with it in PostgreSQL; existing worlds keep their
  nine-territory layout.

### B. Distances and travel

- Keep sortie length reasonable: tune spacing, range and plane speed so a typical mission still
  takes a few minutes (GDD §1.2, continuous participation).
- Recall (GDD §6.4) and landing stay convenient on large maps.
- The mini-map scales and stays readable with up to 16 territories.

### C. Balance and protection

- Revisit raid eligibility, threat levels, AI defenses and world events for larger worlds: more
  players must not mean more raids on any one player.
- New-player protection, shields, pair cooldowns, offline protection and bounty rules keep
  applying per player, not per world.
- The v0.0.15 campaign simulator runs with 10 and 12 simulated players and confirms no runaway
  leader.

### D. Server capacity

- One world of 12 players plus AI traffic and escorts runs within the server tick budget on the
  production server size.
- The existing load test (`npm run loadtest`, `server/loadtest.ts`) is extended to 12 bots on a
  16-territory world.

## 2. New Environments

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Add new miniature settings with their own identity, built on the same art direction and
asset pipeline (GDD §2, §2.1, `docs/ART_DIRECTION.md`).

### A. Environment roster

| Environment | Theme | Example territories and landmarks | Signature obstacles |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classroom (existing) | School desks and supplies | Home Desk, Pencil Point, Notebook Ridge | Pencil cups, rulers |
| Bedroom | Bed, shelves, toys | Pillow Fort, Toy Box, Lamp Tower, Bookshelf Ledge | Lamp cords, toy blocks |
| Library | Tall shelves and reading tables | Encyclopedia Cliffs, Reading Desk, Card Catalogue | Stacked books, ladders |
| Art Room | Easels and paint | Easel Peak, Paint Pot Basin, Clay Table | Easels, drying racks |

### B. Requirements for every environment

- A backdrop, a lighting variant inside the shared lighting model, and themed territory
  platforms with readable ownership colours and symbols.
- A landmark per territory, props and a few obstacles that do not block runways, take-off paths,
  construction slots or stores (tests already check this for Home Desk; extend them).
- Collision boundaries known to the server, so homes can use their landmarks and tall props in
  multiplayer (a v0.0.9 known limitation).
- Synty models where the GDD allows them (furniture, scenery) and custom models where identity
  matters (landmarks, mission targets), using the v0.0.11 import pipeline and outlines.
- Missions, world events and AI flight plans (`src/ai/Navigation.ts`, `src/ai/FlightPlans.ts`)
  work in every environment.

### C. Environment per world

- Each world has an environment chosen when it is created (by the developer for now; v0.0.19's
  campaign lifecycle can rotate them).
- The environment is persisted with the world.

### D. Asset scope

- If licensed packs needed for an environment have not been supplied, keep functional placeholders
  and document the gap. Do not declare an environment complete with placeholders.

## 3. Interest-Based Network Updates

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Send each client what is relevant to it, so bandwidth stays predictable as worlds grow
(GDD §16.4).

### A. Relevance

- Aircraft, projectiles and effects are sent to a client based on distance, line of sight where
  cheap, radar coverage and mission relevance.
- Always relevant: the player's own plane and escorts, raiders on the player's base, mission
  targets, bounty targets and players in a cooperation offer with them.
- Far-away aircraft are sent at a lower rate or as coarse mini-map positions.

### B. Implementation

- Use Colyseus state filtering or per-client channels on top of the schema in `src/net/schema.ts`;
  document the chosen approach in `docs/MULTIPLAYER.md`.
- The server stays authoritative for everything, including what is out of a client's view;
  filtering only affects what is sent.
- Hidden information that would be an advantage (for example raiders outside radar range) is not
  sent to the client at all.

### C. Client behaviour

- Aircraft entering and leaving relevance fade in and out cleanly; interpolation copes with
  rate changes.
- Prediction for the player's own plane is unchanged.

### D. Measurement

- Bandwidth per client with 2, 8 and 12 players on a 16-territory world, before and after, is
  recorded in the README alongside the v0.0.9 measurements.

## 4. Regional Servers and Ping-Based Selection

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Make it possible to run worlds in more than one region and let players pick the one
with the best connection.

### A. Regions

- Server configuration names its region; worlds are created in a region.
- Deployment documentation covers running a second region with its own server process and access
  to the shared database (or a regional database with a documented sync strategy, decided with
  the developer).
- Player identity from v0.0.10 works in every region.

### B. Ping-based selection

- The lobby (v0.0.14) measures latency to each region's server and shows it next to each world.
- The default suggestion is the lowest-latency world with free seats.
- Players can still choose any world (to play with a friend in another region).

### C. Limits

- Automatic world creation, matchmaking and load balancing are v0.0.19. This version only lists
  and connects to existing worlds in several regions.

## 5. Performance, Level of Detail and Streaming

**Priority:** Critical
**Goal:** Keep larger worlds and new environments smooth on modest hardware.

### A. Minimum specification

- Define and document a minimum specification with the developer (for example a recent laptop
  with integrated graphics, 8 GB RAM, Chrome or Edge).
- Target frame rate and frame-time stability on that specification at the Low graphics preset
  (v0.0.8's `src/render/GraphicsSettings.ts`).

### B. Level of detail and culling

- Level-of-detail versions or simplified meshes for props, landmarks and buildings at distance.
- Outline strength and stroke count reduce with distance (the outline renderer already thins
  strokes; extend it to drop them).
- Frustum and distance culling for props, effects and remote aircraft.
- Instancing for repeated props across territories.

### C. Streaming

- Load the selected environment's assets only, with a loading screen (v0.0.14) showing progress.
- Stream distant decoration after the player is in the air where possible.
- Release memory for assets not used by the current world.

### D. Measurement

- Record frame rate, frame time, draw calls, triangles, strokes, memory and load time on the
  minimum specification and on the developer's machine for each environment, using `?perf`.

## 6. Hover and VTOL Flight Mode

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Extend the shared flight model with a hover mode that VTOL jets and helicopters use, so
they fly by the same server-authoritative rules, prediction and controls as every other aircraft.

### A. Flight model

- Add a hover regime to `src/aircraft/FlightModel.ts`: at low speed a hover-capable aircraft holds
  altitude, can climb and descend vertically, yaw on the spot and move slowly in any direction.
- **VTOL jets (Jumper):** transition between hover and normal flight by speed, with a short
  transition band where handling blends. In normal flight they fly like other jets.
- **Helicopters (Wasp, and the Hawk in v0.0.18):** always in the hover regime, with a modest top
  speed, tilt-to-move handling and strong low-altitude stability; no stall.
- Automatic stabilisation and forgiving behaviour (GDD §6.3) apply in hover: releasing the
  controls holds position.
- The same model runs on the server (`server/world/Pilot.ts`) and in client prediction
  (`src/net/Prediction.ts`); prediction tests cover hover.

### B. Take-off and landing

- Hover-capable aircraft take off and land vertically on their home territory without the runway,
  and may land on any flat, clear surface the landing rules accept (`src/aircraft/Landing.ts`),
  never inside another player's territory and never on obstacles.
- Landing away from home gives no repair or rearm unless a rule says so.

### C. Controls

- Keyboard and mouse: the existing controls, with W and S as climb and descend while hovering,
  and A and D as sideways movement.
- Controller: left stick moves, triggers climb and descend in hover, right stick looks, using the
  v0.0.11 input-action layer. No new required buttons.
- Device-aware hints (v0.0.12) show the hover controls when the mode is active.

### D. AI and defenses

- AI pilots (`src/ai/`) can fly hover-capable aircraft for patrols and missions.
- Defenses and AI target hovering aircraft normally; low, slow helicopters are vulnerable to
  ground fire and fighters by design.

### E. Tests

- Flight model tests for hover, transition, vertical landing and stabilisation.
- Prediction and reconciliation tests with hover on a poor network (the v0.0.9 load test
  conditions).
- Landing rule tests: allowed surfaces, forbidden surfaces, other players' territories.

## 7. New Aircraft: Jumper and Wasp

**Priority:** High
**Goal:** Add the Jumper and the Wasp, bringing the roster to twelve, and give the new indoor
environments aircraft that thrive in low-level flight between obstacles.

### A. Roles and handling

| Aircraft | Inspiration | Role | Visual character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jumper | AV-8B | VTOL fighter | Shoulder wings with drooping tips, four swivelling nozzle flaps, hump-backed body |
| Wasp | AH-6 | Light attack helicopter | Small egg-shaped cabin, thin tail boom, paper rotor, side weapon pods |

- **Jumper:** a light fighter that can hover, take off from anywhere suitable and fight at low
  speed. Below average in normal flight; strong close to the ground and among obstacles.
- **Wasp:** cheap, small and nimble, light guns and small rockets or ink pods, very low durability.
  Best at low level in the bedroom, library and art room, ducking behind furniture; easy prey in
  the open sky.

### B. Model requirements

Reuses the aircraft model checklist from v0.0.11:

- Distinctive geometry that reads at speed and at distance, and a stylised folded-paper
  interpretation of the real aircraft, not a replica.
- Visible paper folds and creases.
- Low-poly optimisation within the agreed aircraft triangle budget.
- Consistent outline rendering (aircraft tier, boldest lines).
- Correct collision geometry, shared by client and server.
- Weapon and payload attachment points.
- Folding and construction animation (`src/aircraft/PlaneModel.ts`, `PlaneShapes.ts`).
- Multiplayer synchronisation, including payload and animation state on remote planes.
- Damage and destruction effects (crumple and tear).
- Controller handling tuned with the v0.0.11 input-action layer and analog curves.
- Jumper: nozzle flaps that swivel with the hover transition, synchronised from flight state.
- Wasp: a rotor animation driven by flight state (spinning, tilting with movement), outlined
  without flicker at speed (a simplified blurred disc above a speed threshold).

### C. Balance, cost and mission fit

- Jumper: mid cost; good for defense, patrols and short-notice interceptions from home.
- Wasp: low cost; good for recon among obstacles, harassing defenses, ground-attack support and
  mischief missions; poor against fighters at altitude.
- Mission fit and the director's suitability checks cover low-level and obstacle-heavy missions in
  the new environments.
- Both added to `AircraftId`, `AIRCRAFT_ORDER`, AI roles, server validation and persistence.

### D. Interface and audio integration

- Garage cards and inspector entries, a hover indicator and vertical speed in the HUD using
  v0.0.12 components.
- Jet-nozzle roar changing with the transition for the Jumper, and a paper-flutter rotor for the
  Wasp, in the v0.0.13 audio system.

### E. Tests

- Flight, weapons, costs, mission fit and server validation tests.
- Browser checks of both aircraft flying low through each new environment, with collisions
  matching between client and server.
- A two-player session with one player in a Wasp or Jumper and the other in a jet.

## 8. Testing and Verification

**Priority:** Critical

### A. Automated tests

- Layout validation: every layout has valid homes, runways pointing inward, no overlaps.
- Placement tests: nothing blocks runways, take-off paths, slots or stores in any environment.
- Interest filtering: relevant entities are always sent, hidden ones never leak.
- Persistence: world size and environment survive restarts; existing nine-territory worlds load
  unchanged.
- All earlier tests keep passing.

### B. Load and network tests

- 12 bots on a 16-territory world, with escorts and events, on a normal and a poor network.
- Bandwidth and tick time recorded before and after interest management.

### C. Browser and hardware tests

- Each environment checked in headless Chromium and on the minimum specification and the
  developer's and friend's machines.

### D. Playtest

- The developer and invited friend play a session in each new environment with bots filling a
  large world, and record whether the map feels too empty, too crowded or too slow to cross.

## 11. AI Agent Implementation Strategy

**Priority:** Critical

| Phase | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Audit world generation, networking and rendering cost; measure the baseline |
| 2 | Data-driven layouts; 12- and 16-territory worlds; persistence of world size |
| 3 | Balance and protection review for large worlds; simulator runs |
| 4 | Interest-based network updates and measurements |
| 5 | Bedroom environment |
| 6 | Library and art room environments |
| 7 | Level of detail, culling, instancing and streaming |
| 8 | Regional configuration and ping-based selection |
| 9 | Hover and VTOL flight mode, server and prediction |
| 10 | Jumper and Wasp: stats, models, integration |
| 11 | Load tests, hardware tests, playtest and fixes |

- Work in reviewable increments with a checkpoint for the developer after phases 4, 6 and 11,
  with screenshots of each environment.
- Do not mark a phase complete because the code compiles; verify in the browser, on hardware and
  with load tests.
- Preserve every working feature from earlier versions.

## 12. Definition of Done

- [ ] Worlds of 9, 12 and 16 territories can be created and persist their layout.
- [ ] Up to 12 players and bots play in one world within the server tick budget.
- [ ] Protection, raid and economy rules stay fair in large worlds (simulator and playtest).
- [ ] Bedroom, library and art room environments are complete, or their missing licensed assets are documented.
- [ ] Nothing in any environment blocks runways, slots or stores, and the server knows every collider.
- [ ] Interest-based updates reduce bandwidth, and hidden information is never sent.
- [ ] Worlds can run in more than one region, and the lobby shows ping and suggests the best world.
- [ ] Performance meets the target on the agreed minimum specification.
- [ ] All earlier and new automated tests pass.
- [ ] Measurements and deployment changes are documented in the README and `docs/MULTIPLAYER.md`.
- [ ] The developer and invited friend have played the new environments and their feedback is addressed.
- [ ] The hover and VTOL flight mode works on the server and in prediction, with vertical take-off
      and landing under the landing rules.
- [ ] The Jumper and the Wasp are playable with custom models and suited to low-level flight in
      the new environments.

---

## Final design principle

A bigger world must feel richer, not emptier or slower. Every new environment should be
instantly recognisable as the same paper-and-pencil game, every player should get a good
connection, and the cost of growth should be paid by smarter networking and rendering, never by
the player's frame rate.
